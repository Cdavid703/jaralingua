# Basic English Course 2 — Unit 4: Busy Lives

## Scope

This records the Course Overview teaching page for Unit 4. It follows the pacing guide’s sessions on activities from last night and last week, simple-past questions, recent-time expressions, sound work, and short real-life messages. Practice and assessed work are intentionally planned later in Practice Lab.

## Unit outcome

By the end of the unit, a learner can describe completed actions from last night or last week, ask and answer a simple-past yes/no question, make a negative statement, and organize a short sequence of past events.

## New language

- Simple past affirmative sentences.
- Regular-past spelling: `-ed`, final `-e + d`, consonant doubling, and consonant + `y → ied`.
- High-frequency irregular forms: went, had, did, got, saw, ate, made, came.
- `-ed` sound awareness: `/t/`, `/d/`, `/ɪd/`.
- Yes/no questions: `Did + subject + base verb ...?`
- Short answers and negatives: `Yes, I did.`, `No, I didn't.`, and `didn't + base verb`.
- Recent time: last night, yesterday, last week, on Monday, two days ago.
- Sequencing: first, then, after that, finally.
- Contextual expressions: woke up, hung out, came over, picked up, be on the go, call it a night.

## Boundaries

The page does not reteach present simple versus present continuous (Unit 1), shopping demonstratives/prices (Unit 2), or comparatives/superlatives (Unit 3). It makes the new choice explicit: use the past for an action that started and finished before now.

## Pedagogical sequence

1. **Time before form:** identify the completed past moment.
2. **Meaningful vocabulary:** select adult-life actions at home, work, study, or social time.
3. **Core sentence:** build `subject + past verb + time`.
4. **Accuracy:** distinguish regular spelling patterns and a concise irregular-verb set.
5. **Sound awareness:** separate written `-ed` from its three common pronunciations.
6. **Interaction:** ask with `Did + base verb`, answer briefly, and form negatives with `didn't + base verb`.
7. **Connected discourse:** use sequencing language to tell a four-event busy-life story.
8. **Transfer:** identify the same grammar in a diary entry, voicemail, and WhatsApp reply.
9. **Naturalness:** introduce limited phrasal verbs and idioms with Spanish approximation and contextual examples.
10. **Self-check:** close with specific “I can” outcomes before future Practice Lab activities.

## -ed pronunciation teaching block

The Unit 4 explanation page now integrates the pronunciation block into Topic 04, Regular verbs: spelling and pronunciation with -ed.

- It teaches the physical reason for the three sounds: a voiceless final base-verb sound leads to `/t/`; a vowel or voiced final sound leads to `/d/`; and final `/t/` or `/d/` requires an extra `/ɪd/` syllable.
- The learner is explicitly told to listen to the last **sound**, not simply the final spelling. `laughed` is used as the key counterexample: its final sound is `/f/`, therefore `-ed` is `/t/`.
- Each category includes the rule, a throat-vibration check, a contrast model, and six individual clickable verbs.
- Twenty-one real ElevenLabs MP3 files are stored in `ingles/basico-2/audio/unit4/pronunciation/ed-endings/`: 18 word models and 3 category contrasts.
- Controls offer 0.75× and 1.0× playback. No browser speech-synthesis fallback is used.
- The entire Unit 4 page, including this topic, remains closed by default; students choose when to open it.
