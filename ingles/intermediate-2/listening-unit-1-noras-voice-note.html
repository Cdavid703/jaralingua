Vocabulary preview

a bit serious

A tactful way to say that someone initially seems formal or not very relaxed.

old classmate

A person who studied in the same class as you in the past.

get back in touch

Communicate again after a period without contact.

new contact

A person you met recently and may communicate with again.

hit it off

Like each other quickly and communicate easily.

Language noticing

Hear what each clause is doing

Mr. Okafor is the neighbor who lives across the hall.

Why who?

The clause identifies a person. Who is the safest and most natural choice for people.

Iris, who was in my evening class, surprised me.

Why commas?

The name already identifies Iris. The clause adds extra information, so it uses commas and who, never that.

She is an old classmate that I lost contact with.

Can who replace that?

Yes. This is an identifying clause about a person without commas. That is common in conversation; who is also correct.

We hit it off because we both enjoy city stories.

What relationship signal do you hear?

Hit it off says that two people like each other quickly and communicate easily.

Open Nora's complete transcript

Hi, I just got home from Mr. Okafor's Saturday dinner, and I have to tell you about it. Mr. Okafor is the neighbor who lives across the hall. He seemed a bit serious at first, but he is actually the person who helps everyone in the building meet each other.

The biggest surprise was Iris, who was in my evening class years ago. She is an old classmate that I lost contact with when I changed jobs. We got back in touch tonight, and it felt completely natural.

I also met Gabriel, a photographer who recently moved into the building. He is the new contact that I talked to most during dinner. We hit it off because we both enjoy collecting stories about cities and people. He was kind of quiet at first, but once we started talking about photography, he became really enthusiastic.

So one dinner changed my social circle. I reconnected with someone from my past, got to know a new neighbor, and found two people I genuinely get on well with. Iris and I are meeting for coffee next week, and Gabriel and I promised to keep in touch.
