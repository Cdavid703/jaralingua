Your final oral assessment

Your final oral assessment · Live News Report

Choose and explain one story

Create a fictional news programme about one natural disaster or news event from the eight choices in “Choose your news story” below. Explain what happened, where and when, its effects and people’s response. Interview someone who experienced the event.

Every student speaks

  • 2 students: A plays the presenter/studio journalist, then the witness; B plays the field reporter. Announce the role change. A returns to the studio for the closing.
  • 3 students: presenter/studio journalist, field reporter and witness.
  • 4 students: presenter, studio journalist, field reporter and witness.

Speaking time: 2 students: 4–6 minutes · 3: 6–8 minutes · 4: 8–10 minutes. Share the time fairly. The presenter must also ask questions and summarise information.

Build your news report

  • A programme introduction, a headline and a closing.
  • News from the studio and a connection to the place of the event.
  • An interview: what the witness was doing, what happened, feelings and people’s response.

Use the Unit 6 language

  • At least four different verb + preposition combinations across the team. Everyone uses at least one.
  • At least one reported statement per student. The witness can report a family member’s words.
  • A follow-up question based on a real answer from your partner.

Speak. Do not read.

No scripts, phones, written sentences or teleprompters during the assessment. Prepare with a map of ideas, then rehearse without it. Images can help you remember the story. Use simple English you understand and have practised in class. Your teacher may ask a short follow-up question.

Your final oral grade is individual, even though you perform in a team. See the assessment criteria below.

Your choice · Unit 6

Choose your news story

Choose one of these eight topics from Unit 6. Invent the place, people and details. The flood video is a model; your team can choose a different story.

Flood Inundación

Water enters homes after heavy rain. Interview a resident about leaving home.

Useful words: heavy rain · rescue crew · shelter · clean water

Try: looking for people · asking for help

Earthquake Terremoto

The ground shakes and damages buildings. Interview a resident about what they were doing.

Useful words: ground · damaged buildings · rescue crew · safe

Try: looking at the damage · listening to instructions

Hurricane Huracán

Strong winds and heavy rain hit a coastal town. Interview a resident about the storm.

Useful words: strong winds · heavy rain · fallen tree · shelter

Try: warning about the storm · asking for supplies

Drought Sequía

A long period without enough rain reduces the town’s water supply. Interview a resident about daily life.

Useful words: little rain · water levels · clean water · supplies

Try: talking about water · asking for help

Wildfire Incendio forestal

A fire spreads through a wooded area. Interview a resident who left home.

Useful words: fire · smoke · damage · shelter

Try: warning about the fire · caring for people

Landslide Deslizamiento de tierra

Soil and rocks block a mountain road. Interview a resident about how their journey changed.

Useful words: rocks · soil · blocked road · heavy rain

Try: looking at the road · listening to updates

A council repair announcement Anuncio del concejo sobre reparaciones

The council announces repairs to the community library. Interview a resident about the closure and the plan.

Useful words: council · spokesperson · repair plan · statement

Try: asking about repairs · talking about the plan

A concert announcement Anuncio de un concierto

A singer announces a concert. Interview a fan who heard the announcement about their reaction.

Useful words: singer · concert · announcement · source

Try: listening to the singer · talking about the concert

For a disaster, interview a witness or resident. For another event, interview a participant, fan or resident who experienced it. Ask what they were doing, what happened and how they felt. Then report what they said.

Classroom demonstration · assign the roles

Who plays each role?

The teacher can read David’s part and invite students to play the other roles. Names are kept for this visit only.

The demonstration uses one presenter’s image for two different characters: David in the studio and Leo at the location. Students assign these roles to their own team members.

Scenes 1–3 include example videos. Perform the interview and closing live. You may read the model during this demonstration; you may not read during the final assessment.

Notice → try → use

Make the language work for you

Use the whole combination

  • Families are asking for help.
  • Rescuers are looking for people.
  • They are listening to instructions.
  • Helpers are caring for injured people.

Your turn: describe your picture with two combinations.

Report someone’s message

Ana says: “We are safe, but we need clean water.”

Later, Leo reports: Ana said that they were safe, but they needed clean water.

we → they  ·  are → were  ·  need → needed

Keep Ana’s meaning. Change the words to fit Leo’s report.

Try reported speech

Ana: “We are asking for blankets.”

Start: Ana said that…

Show a possible answer

Ana said that they were asking for blankets.

Another way: Ana told me that they were asking for blankets.

Use it for another news event

Concert: The singer is talking about her new show. Fans are listening to her announcement.

Singer: “I can perform on Saturday.”

Reporter: The singer said that she could perform on Saturday.

The same language works with a different story.

Send these through the channel your teacher gives you.

Images and video

Prepare a studio image, an image of your chosen event and an interview image. Send your teacher a prompt for one short video of the event. The teacher plays that clip when your studio journalist introduces the news. Your team speaks live; the clip should have no dialogue, only optional quiet ambient sound.

Plan a clip of about eight seconds. Review it before rehearsing and describe what it actually shows. The voiced videos on this page are classroom examples; they do not replace your live speaking in the assessment.

Optional image prompt · keep your identity

Attach your own photo, or a photo you have permission to use. Replace the bracketed scene description.

Use the attached photograph as the identity reference. Preserve the person’s recognizable facial identity, face proportions, eyes, eyebrows, nose, hairline, hairstyle, beard if present, skin tone and apparent age. Do not beautify, reshape or replace their face. Create a photorealistic horizontal 16:9 image of this person [at a news desk / reporting at the chosen event / interviewing a participant]. Keep the face clearly visible. Change only the clothing, setting and pose needed for the scene. No text, logos or watermark.

Check the result against the original photo. A prompt cannot guarantee an identical face. Regenerate it if the person’s features change.

Keep the same clothes and location between related scenes. Use the same witness reference image to preserve their appearance.

How your teacher assesses you · individual score /50

Task completion · /10

Deliver your part of a clear news report, including the required situation and role.

Interaction · /10

Listen, take turns, ask and answer questions, and respond to what others say.

Fluency · /10

Keep the message moving. Natural pauses and self-correction are acceptable.

Vocabulary and structures · /10

Use familiar Unit 6 news or disaster vocabulary, past tenses, verb + preposition combinations and reported speech with clear meaning.

Pronunciation · /10

Speak clearly enough to be understood. You do not need to imitate a native accent.

Scale for each criterion: 1–3 Weak · 4–5 Low · 6–7 Fair · 8–9 Proficient · 10 Exemplary. The quality of AI images or video does not earn language points.

Before you finish rehearsing

Can each person explain the story without reading? Does the reported speech match the witness’s words? Did everyone ask or answer a question? Choose one thing to improve and perform that part again.
