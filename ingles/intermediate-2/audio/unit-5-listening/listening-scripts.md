# Unit 5 · A Different First Impression

## A Different First Impression
File: `a-different-first-impression.mp3`

Sam: Maya, are we ready for the community food collection? Look at Alex by the door. He is sitting alone with a notebook. He looks bored.

Maya: He might simply be concentrating. Being quiet does not always mean someone is bored.

Sam: That is possible, but he has not spoken to anyone. Do you know him?

Maya: Yes. This is his first time volunteering. He told me he is checking the guest list and trying to learn everyone’s names.

Sam: Oh, that changes my impression. And you must be tired. You have been working since six this morning.

Maya: A little. At first, I felt confused because the instructions were confusing. But we organized the supplies together, so I feel calmer now.

Sam: What about that poster? It says, Coffee cups demand a holiday. That can’t be a real news report.

Maya: It is satire. Cups cannot ask for holidays. The joke exaggerates how busy we have been preparing everything.

Sam: I see. The poster is funny, although the work is tiring. Shall I invite Alex to help us with the boxes?

Maya: Good idea. A friendly question can help break the ice. Ask him what he enjoys doing.

Sam: I will. I thought he was not interested, but now I want to hear his story.

Maya: Exactly. Notice the clues, consider another explanation, and talk to the person before you decide.
