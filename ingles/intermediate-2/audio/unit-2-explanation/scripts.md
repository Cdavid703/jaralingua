## Wish, hope, dream, or goal?
File: `wish-hope-dream-goal.mp3`

I hope I get the scholarship because the result is still possible. I dream of managing community projects because that is an important long-term aspiration. I wish I had more experience because my present reality is different from what I want. My goal is to complete a project-management certificate this year because a goal can be organized into practical steps. A hope is possible, a dream is meaningful, a wish often contrasts with reality, and a goal can become a plan.

## Present wishes
File: `present-wishes.mp3`

I wish I lived closer to campus. This is a present wish, even though lived has a past form. The past form creates distance from reality: I do not live close now. I wish I had more free time means that I do not have enough free time. With the verb be, were is the safest standard form. I wish I were more confident. Was is common in informal conversation, but were clearly marks the hypothetical meaning.

## Wishes about ability
File: `wish-could.mp3`

Listen to the difference. I can attend the workshop, so the possibility exists now. I hope I can attend next month, so the future possibility is still open. I wish I could attend the workshop, so a present condition prevents me from attending. After could, use the base verb without to. Say, I wish I could travel. Do not say, I wish I can travel, or I wish I could to travel.

## Future wishes and complaints
File: `future-wishes-complaints.mp3`

I hope the bus arrives on time is a neutral future hope. I wish the bus would arrive on time for once sounds like a complaint about a repeated problem. Wish plus would focuses on a desired change in behavior or in a situation outside the speaker's control. I wish my coworker would answer my messages. For your own ordinary decision, use responsibility or hope instead. Say, I should study more consistently, not, I wish I would study more.

## Past wishes and regrets
File: `past-wishes-regrets.mp3`

I regret turning down the internship. This directly names the regretted action. I wish I had accepted the internship. This imagines the opposite past. I should have asked more questions. This evaluates the better action that I did not take. The past is finished, so wish takes the past perfect: had plus the past participle. Remember that should've may sound like should of, but the correct written form is always should have.

## Second conditional
File: `second-conditional.mp3`

What would you do if you found a wallet on campus? If I found it, I would take it to security. I could also contact the office, and the owner might return to look for it. In the second conditional, the if clause uses a past form, and the result uses would, could, or might plus the base verb. Say, If I had time, I would help. Do not normally put would inside the if clause.

## Reasoning through dilemmas
File: `reasoning-through-dilemmas.mp3`

A dilemma is more than a problem. It is a difficult choice in which every option has a cost or a competing value. Before deciding, identify the facts and the people affected. Name the realistic options and predict their consequences. Then consider the values involved, such as honesty, fairness, loyalty, privacy, or responsibility. A reasoned opinion might say: I understand both sides, but I would probably report the mistake because accurate information protects everyone affected.

## Advice strength
File: `advice-strength.mp3`

You could talk to the academic advisor. That offers one low-pressure option. You might want to compare the deadlines first. That sounds careful and diplomatic. You should explain the situation honestly. That is a clear recommendation. If I were you, I would ask for more information. That gives a personal hypothetical perspective. You had better submit the form today. That is a strong warning because delay may cause a negative consequence.

## Responding to advice
File: `responding-to-advice.mp3`

If I were you, I would speak to the department before rejecting the offer. That makes sense, but I would need a flexible schedule. Do you think I should explain that in my email? Yes. You could describe the conflict and ask whether another schedule is available. That is a good point. I had not considered asking for an alternative. A natural response acknowledges the advice, adds a real constraint, and keeps the conversation moving.

## Unit 2 vocabulary
File: `unit-2-vocabulary.mp3`

Samira's ambition is to lead a community organization, and earning a graduate certificate is an achievable goal. The scholarship is a valuable opportunity, but the travel cost is a serious obstacle. She has two options and must consider the consequences of each one. A hybrid schedule could be a practical compromise. Before making a decision, she will reconsider her priorities and decide whether she can accept the responsibility that comes with the new role.

## Decision phrasal verbs
File: `decision-phrasal-verbs.mp3`

Before you go for an opportunity, think it over carefully. Do not give up an important goal because of one obstacle. You may be able to work out a practical solution. Even if the plan does not work out exactly as expected, you could end up discovering a better option. Later, when you look back on the decision, focus on what you learned as well as on the final result.

## Fixed expressions and idioms
File: `fixed-expressions-idioms.mp3`

First, the fixed expressions. Make a wish, pursue a dream, achieve a goal, face a dilemma, weigh the consequences, and live with a decision. Now the idioms. If you are at a crossroads, you must choose an important direction. A dream come true is a deep desire that becomes real. If you have second thoughts, you begin to doubt your decision. If you learn the hard way, an unpleasant experience teaches you an important lesson.

## Unit 2 sound practice
File: `unit-2-sound-practice.mp3`

Listen to I'd. I'd accept the offer means I would accept the offer because accept is a base verb. I wish I'd asked first means I wish I had asked first because asked is a past participle. Now connect would you: What would you do? In natural speech, should have is often reduced: I should've waited. The spelling is still should have. Finally, hear the last sound: good advice ends in s, but I advise you ends in z.

## Unit 2 integrated model
File: `unit-2-integrated-model.mp3`

Valentina hopes she gets a scholarship for a program in another city. She dreams of managing community projects, but she is at a crossroads. I wish I were more certain, she tells her counselor. If I accepted the scholarship, I would have to leave my current job. What would you do if you were in my position? The counselor replies, If I were you, I would think the offer over. You could weigh the financial consequences, and you might want to ask whether the program offers part-time work. Valentina answers, That makes sense. I regret turning down a similar opportunity two years ago. I wish I had asked for more information before I decided. I should have compared all the options. After the meeting, she gathers the facts before answering. If the schedule worked out, she thinks, I could accept the scholarship without giving up my job completely.
