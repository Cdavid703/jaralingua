## Relative clauses: who and that

File: `relative-clauses-who-that.mp3`

Sofia is the neighbor who helped me when I moved in. Sofia is the neighbor that helped me when I moved in. Both sentences identify a person, and both are grammatically correct. The difference is not the action. Who is the clearest and safest choice for a person. That is a common alternative in a defining clause without commas. Now listen to the contrast: Sofia, who helped me when I moved in, lives upstairs. The commas show extra information, so that is not possible.

## Social-circle vocabulary

File: `social-circle-vocabulary.mp3`

Mr. Okafor is a trustworthy neighbor who does people favors. Lina is a close friend who never shares private gossip. Owen is a coworker that I met during a community project. My neighborhood can be a little noisy, but people usually solve conflict respectfully. A nosy person asks about private matters, while a friendly neighbor shows interest without crossing a boundary.

## Tactful descriptions

File: `tactful-descriptions.mp3`

My new coworker is kind of quiet at first, but she is very friendly. One of my neighbors can be a bit direct in group chats. The building is a little bit noisy on weekends, but it is usually peaceful. Kind of sounds conversational. A bit often softens a mildly negative adjective. A little bit can sound gentler and more careful.

## Dating and meeting people

File: `dating-meeting-people.mp3`

People meet through friends, at work, in classes, at community events, and through dating apps. Someone may ask another person out, go for coffee, have dinner, take a walk, and decide whether to meet again. Chivalry describes traditional polite behavior, but expectations vary across people and cultures. A fling is a short, casual relationship. A one-night stand means one sexual encounter, so use that adult expression carefully and only when the context requires it.

## Phrasal verbs

File: `relationship-phrasal-verbs.mp3`

I met up with Elena after class, and we caught up over coffee. We get on well because we respect each other's ideas. She introduced me to Marcus, and we hit it off immediately. A few weeks later, Marcus asked Elena out. These are phrasal verbs: meet up, catch up, get on well, hit it off, and ask out.

## Fixed relationship expressions

File: `fixed-relationship-expressions.mp3`

We got to know each other during a community project and discovered that we had a lot in common. After the project, we kept in touch through voice messages. Then work became busy, and we lost touch for almost a year. Last month, we got back in touch. These are fixed relationship expressions, not all phrasal verbs.

## Relationship idioms

File: `relationship-idioms.mp3`

Good friends do not have to see eye to eye on everything. A loyal friend supports you through thick and thin and can be a shoulder to cry on when life is difficult. A fair-weather friend, however, is present only when life is easy. These are idioms because their complete meaning is not simply the sum of each individual word.

## Sound practice

File: `unit-1-sound-practice.mp3`

Listen to who: the friend who called. Hold the long vowel in who. Now listen to that: the friend that called. Place your tongue gently between your teeth for the voiced th sound. Link the final consonant to the next vowel: get on well, keep in touch. Reduce to in natural speech: get to know. Finally, hear the comma pauses: Iris, who was in my evening class, is an old friend.

## Integrated model

File: `unit-1-integrated-model.mp3`

One of the most important people in my social circle is Camila, who lives two doors down. She is a trustworthy neighbor who helped me when I first moved into the building. At first, she seemed kind of reserved, but once we got to know each other, we discovered that we had a lot in common. We hit it off, and now we get on very well. Camila later introduced me to Rafael, the coworker that organized our neighborhood fundraiser. He was a little bit quiet at first, but he eventually asked her out for coffee. They are seeing each other now. Camila is also a shoulder to cry on, and I know she will support me through thick and thin. Even when we are busy, we keep in touch.
