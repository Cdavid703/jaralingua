## Technology basics
File: `technology-basics.mp3`

My tablet will not connect to the Wi-Fi when I open the learning platform. The screen is not frozen, but the page keeps loading and the app sometimes crashes. A useful technical report names the device, the symptom, and the moment when the problem happens. Instead of saying, My tablet is bad, say, My tablet will not connect to the Wi-Fi when I open the learning platform.

## Embedded questions
File: `embedded-questions.mp3`

A direct question is, Where is the Wi-Fi setting? To make it more polite, say, Could you tell me where the Wi-Fi setting is? After could you tell me, use statement order: subject before verb. Do you know if the update is available? is a yes or no embedded question. Do not say, Could you tell me where is the setting? or, Do you know where do I save the file?

## Device functions and instructions
File: `device-functions-and-instructions.mp3`

You use a cloud drive to store files. To give clear instructions, use one action at a time. First, connect the device to power. Then, press the start button. Finally, check the settings. Use do not for an important negative instruction: Do not disconnect the cable during the update. Clear instructions tell a person what the device does and what to do next.

## Technology phrasal verbs
File: `technology-phrasal-verbs.mp3`

Hook up the monitor to the laptop. Look up the error code online. The antenna cannot pick up a signal. Do not put off the security update. Turn down the volume. Do not take the device apart without guidance. With a noun object, you can say turn down the volume or turn the volume down. With a pronoun, the pronoun goes in the middle: turn it down, look it up, and take it apart.

## Balanced technology comparisons
File: `balanced-technology-comparisons.mp3`

On the one hand, public Wi-Fi is convenient because it helps us connect when we are away from home. On the other hand, it may not be secure, so we should avoid using it for banking or sensitive information. This is a balanced comparison because both sides discuss the same decision. The expression does not mean that you have no opinion. It means that you show a benefit and a limitation before you decide.

## Sensitive information
File: `sensitive-information.mp3`

Personal information can identify a person. Sensitive information can open or control an account. Passwords, PINs, and bank details are sensitive information. A strong password is unique and difficult to guess. Before you share information, ask who is requesting it, why they need it, and whether you can verify the request through an official website or app. Never enter real passwords or PINs in a class activity or an unexpected message.

## Scams and malware
File: `scams-and-malware.mp3`

A scam tries to take money or information. A phishing message may look like it comes from a trusted company. A Trojan looks safe but installs harmful software, and a virus can damage or spread through files. A warning sign is urgency: Act now or your account will close. Another warning sign is a request for a password or PIN. Do not click the link. Open the official website or app yourself and verify the message there.

## Digital-safety advice
File: `digital-safety-advice.mp3`

Use should for advice: You should use a unique password. Use must for an important requirement: You must protect your PIN. Use must not for a prohibition: You must not share your password with an unexpected message. After these modal verbs, use the base verb without to. For a general safety condition, say: If a message asks for your password, do not reply. If you receive a suspicious link, report it.
