01Technology basics: name the problem firstUse precise vocabulary to explain what is happening before you ask for help.+
Real situation

A helpful support conversation starts with the problem, not with “It doesn’t work.”

Compare My phone has a problem with My screen is frozen and the app keeps crashing when I open the file. The second sentence gives a listener enough information to help.

screen is frozen

The image stays still and the device does not respond.

My screen is frozen.

app keeps crashing

The app closes or stops repeatedly.

The app keeps crashing.

cannot connect

The device cannot join a network or service.

I can’t connect to the Wi-Fi.

battery is low

The device needs power soon.

The battery is running low.

Useful report

Give three pieces of information

Device

What is affected?

My laptop is not responding.

Symptom

What happens?

The page keeps loading.

Context

When does it happen?

It happens when I try to upload a file.

Too general

My computer is bad.

My computer won’t turn on.

Name the observable problem.

Wrong preposition

I can’t connect the Wi-Fi.

I can’t connect to the Wi-Fi.

Connect to a network or service.

02Ask for help with embedded questionsSound polite and keep statement word order after the question opener.+
Grammar focus

An embedded question contains a question inside a longer, more polite request.

Start with Could you tell me…?, Do you know…?, or Can you show me…?. After that opener, the rest uses statement order: subject before verb.

Direct question

Where is the Wi-Fi setting?

Useful with a friend, but direct.

Embedded question

Could you tell me where the Wi-Fi setting is?

Polite and natural for technical support.

Yes / no information

Do you know if the update is available?

Use if or whether, not a question-word.

Form

Build the sentence in two steps

Step 1

Choose a polite opener

Could you tell me + question word…?

Could you tell me how…?

Step 2

Use statement order

question word + subject + verb

…how I can reset the password?

Question order after the opener

Could you tell me where is the setting?

Could you tell me where the setting is?

Do not invert the verb and subject inside the embedded question.

Using do after the opener

Do you know where do I save the file?

Do you know where I save the file?

The opener already carries the question structure.

03How technology works: functions and instructionsExplain what a device does, then guide another person through a short process.+
Communicative goal

Good instructions answer two questions: what is this for, and what should I do next?

Use to + verb to explain purpose, then use clear imperatives and sequence words to explain a procedure.

Purpose

use + device + to + verb

You use a cloud drive to store files.

Instructions

imperative + object

Press the power button.
Do not disconnect the cable.

Sequence

first → then → finally

First, connect the charger. Then, restart the device.

Manual language

Keep one action in each instruction

Unclear

First you can maybe connect it and then the button.

Clear

First, connect the device to power. Then, press the start button.

04Phrasal verbs: action and word orderUse six common technology actions and place pronouns in the correct position.+
Grammar focus

These phrasal verbs can separate when the object is a noun. A pronoun must go in the middle.

Say turn down the volume or turn the volume down. With it, say only turn it down.

hook upconnect

Hook up the monitor to the laptop.

look upsearch for information

Look up the error code online.

pick upreceive / detect

The antenna cannot pick up a signal.

put offdelay

Do not put off the security update.

turn downreduce

Turn down the volume.

take apartseparate into pieces

Do not take the device apart without guidance.

Pronoun after the particle

Turn down it.

Turn it down.

With a pronoun, the object goes between the verb and particle.

Unsafe instruction

Take apart it before checking the manual.

Do not take it apart before checking the manual.

Correct grammar can also carry a useful safety message.

05Compare technology fairlyUse “on the one hand / on the other hand” to present two relevant sides of a decision.+
Reasoning language

A balanced comparison does not mean you have no opinion. It means you show the benefit and the limitation before you decide.

Use the pair to connect two contrasting, relevant ideas about the same topic.

Balanced comparison

On the one hand, cloud storage makes files easy to access. On the other hand, you need a secure password.

Not a contrast

On the one hand, my phone is new. On the other hand, I like music.

The two ideas must answer the same decision or topic.

06Identity theft: protect sensitive informationDistinguish ordinary information from details that should never be shared casually.+
Safety concept

Identity theft happens when someone uses private information to pretend to be you or access your accounts.

In this course, use fictional examples only. Never enter a real password, PIN, bank number, or account detail into an activity or a message.

personal information

Information that identifies a person, such as a full name or address.

sensitive information

Details that can open or control an account: passwords, PINs, or bank details.

secure network

A trusted connection protected by proper security.

strong password

A unique, long password that is difficult to guess.

Decision rule

Pause before sharing

Ask

Who is requesting it?

Verify the sender through a trusted route, not the message link.

Ask

Why do they need it?

A real service should explain the purpose clearly.

Act

Do not share secrets

Passwords and PINs are not needed to “verify” a surprise message.

07Scams, Trojans, and viruses: notice the red flagsRecognize pressure, suspicious links, and requests for information before you react.+
Critical reading

A suspicious message often tries to make you act before you have time to check.

Scams may promise money, threaten an account closure, or imitate a job offer. Malware, including Trojans and viruses, can enter through unsafe files, links, or downloads.

scamdeceptive plan

A scam tries to take money or information.

phishingfalse message

A phishing message imitates a trusted organization.

Trojanhidden harmful software

A Trojan looks safe but installs harmful software.

virusharmful program

A virus can damage or spread through files.

Red flag

“Act now or your account will close. Send your PIN to confirm.”

Urgency plus a request for a secret is a reason to stop and verify.

Safer response

“I will open the official app myself and check the account there.”

Use a trusted route instead of the message link.

08Give digital-safety advice clearlyMatch the grammar to the strength of the recommendation, warning, or rule.+
Grammar focus

Use modal verbs to show whether you are offering advice, stating an important rule, or warning someone not to act.

These choices are not interchangeable. Should gives advice; must states a strong requirement; mustn’t marks a prohibition.

Advice

should / shouldn’t + base verb

You should use a unique password.

Requirement

must + base verb

You must protect your PIN.

Prohibition

mustn’t + base verb

You mustn’t share your password.

Safety condition

Use an if-clause to connect a risk and an action

Clear prevention

If a message asks for your password, do not reply.

Correct form

If you receive a suspicious link, report it.

After if, use the present form for this general safety rule.

Modal + to

You should to update the app.

You should update the app.

Modals take the base verb without to.

Weak response to a secret request

You could share your PIN if the message looks official.

You mustn’t share your PIN.

A PIN is sensitive information.

09Put it together: one useful conversationCombine a technical problem, a polite request, clear steps, and a safe digital decision.+
Transfer task

You are ready when you can move through the whole situation, not only repeat one grammar rule.

Read or listen to the model, then use the checklist to make your own short response about a real or imaginary device problem.

1 · ReportMy laptop keeps freezing when I join a video meeting.
2 · AskCould you tell me how I can check the connection?
3 · GuideFirst, look up the network settings. Then, turn the device off and on again.
4 · ProtectOn the one hand, public Wi-Fi is convenient. On the other hand, you mustn’t use it to share sensitive information.
