Unit Passport

Unit 1: Relationships: Neighbors, Friends and Meeting People

Big idea: from first contact to real connection.

This unit teaches students how to identify people, describe relationships, speak tactfully about personality and behavior, and explain how people meet, reconnect and stay in touch.

Learning Outcomes

  • Describe relationships with neighbors, friends and people they meet.
  • Explain who a person is using relative clauses with who and that.
  • Use softeners to describe personality and behavior with tact.
  • Talk about friendship, contact, dating and social connection.

Grammar and Functions

  • who for identifying people: "She's the neighbor who helped me."
  • that for people or things in common speech: "It's an app that helps people meet."
  • kind of, a bit, a little bit to soften descriptions.
  • Polite description instead of blunt judgment.

Teaching Route

How Unit 1 will be taught

01

Start with people, not grammar

Open with real social questions: who students get on well with, who they keep in touch with, and how they usually meet people.

02

Build a relationship map

Organize neighbor, close friend, old friend, classmate, date, coworker and new contact as a visual social map.

03

Use relative clauses as a real tool

Teach who and that as the answer to a communication problem: "Which person do you mean?"

04

Practice tactful description

Move from blunt comments to natural English: "He can be a bit direct" instead of "He is rude."

05

Turn phrasal verbs into a story

Follow the relationship sequence: get to know someone, hit it off, have a lot in common, get on well, keep in touch, catch up, get back in touch.

06

Close with production readiness

Prepare students to describe a person, compare ways of meeting people, or explain the history of a friendship.

Teacher Focus

Keep the theory visible but light. The lesson should feel like a communication unit first, then a grammar unit. Grammar appears when students need it to identify people, avoid repetition and sound more natural.

Student Experience

Students see a clear unit path from their phone: what the unit is about, what language matters, and how the language helps them talk about real relationships.
