Course guide

What Unit 6 teaches

The official unit moves from future plans to practical advice and a reorganized life plan.

+

Central learning goal

Students learn to communicate a plan, identify what is already arranged, recognize a conflict, consider advice, and explain a final decision.

This is more than memorizing future forms. The language changes according to how certain a plan is, whether details are confirmed, and how strong a recommendation should sound.

Teaching boundary: organizing life is presented as practical peer planning. The page does not imitate psychological diagnosis or clinical counseling.

A concrete context

Olivia and Marcus make the grammar visible through intentions, appointments, conflicts, advice, and final decisions.

Official focusMeaningCore languageLearning outcome
Making future plans Describe intentions and detailed arrangements. be going to, present continuous for arrangements Explain what a person plans to do and what is already scheduled.
Problems and advice Offer options and recommendations without sounding rude or absolute. could, should, ought to Match the strength of the advice to the seriousness of the situation.
Organizing life Respond to advice, explain limitations, and choose a realistic solution. That might work, I'd rather, I could Describe a problem-to-decision process clearly and respectfully.

Input story

Olivia's overbooked month

A realistic schedule gives every future form and piece of advice a clear purpose.

+

The people

Olivia Reed is a singer-songwriter preparing a new release. She wants to record, rehearse, promote her music, spend time with her family, and protect her health.

Marcus Chen coordinates her professional schedule. He does not make every decision for her. He identifies conflicts, offers options, confirms details, and helps communicate the final agenda.

The problem

Olivia's intentions are reasonable individually, but several appointments overlap. Some events are confirmed, some are only ideas, and some are necessary. The language must show these differences.

What the image reveals

Many colored notes do not automatically create a good plan. Priority, certainty, travel time, and recovery time matter.

10:00Producer meeting
12:00Recording session with no lunch break
2:30Radio interview with insufficient travel time
6:30Family visit

What must be distinguished

PlanOlivia is going to record a new song.
FixedShe is meeting the producer at ten.
OptionShe could move the interview.
NeedShe has to leave by five.
Meaning first

One future, different viewpoints

English future forms describe intention, arrangement, schedule, or a decision made now.

+

Why English has several future forms

The event may happen later, but the grammar tells the listener how the speaker sees that event now. A plan in someone's mind is different from an appointment with a confirmed time. An official timetable is different from a decision made during the conversation.

Before speaking

Intention already exists

Olivia is going to reduce her workload. The decision is already in her mind.

Details confirmed

Arrangement is concrete

She is meeting Marcus at nine. The time and person are fixed.

External schedule

Timetable controls it

The train leaves at six. The schedule exists independently of Olivia's plan.

During the conversation

Decision happens now

I will call the studio now. Marcus decides while he is speaking.

Intentionbe going to

A plan already exists, but every practical detail may not be fixed.

Arrangementpresent continuous

The event has concrete details such as a person, time, place, or booking.

Official schedulesimple present

A timetable or institution determines the time.

Decision nowwill

The speaker decides, offers, or promises at the moment of speaking.

Intention

Olivia is going to record a new song.

The recording is part of her plan. The sentence does not confirm a specific time.

Arrangement

Olivia is recording at noon on Thursday.

The recording has a scheduled time. Other people and resources are probably involved.

Timetable

The studio opens at nine.

This is an official or regular schedule, not Olivia's personal intention.

Decision now

I will call the studio now.

Marcus chooses this action while he is speaking.

Common oversimplification

Weak explanation: Use will for every future sentence.
Accurate explanation: Choose the form according to intention, confirmed arrangement, official schedule, or immediate decision.
Why: The forms can refer to similar future events, but they communicate different degrees and types of planning.

Grammar 1

Plans and intentions with be going to

Use this structure when the plan already exists before the speaker mentions it.

+

Core meaning

Be going to presents a future intention or a plan that has already been considered. It does not automatically mean that every appointment is confirmed.

subject + am / is / are + going to + base verb
I am going to protect Friday evening. The speaker already has this intention.
Olivia is going to record a new song. Recording is part of her plan.
Marcus and Olivia are going to reorganize the month. They have decided to do it.

Plan versus arrangement

Olivia is going to rehearse more this month. Today, she is rehearsing with the band at four. The second sentence has a confirmed detail.

Affirmative

subject + be + going to + base verb
She is going to visit her mother.
They are going to reduce the number of interviews.

Negative

subject + be + not + going to + base verb
She is not going to accept another event.
We are not going to cancel the concert.

Yes / no question form

be + subject + going to + base verb?
Is Olivia going to travel in June?
Are they going to change the interview?

Information question form

question word + be + subject + going to + base verb?
When is she going to rest?
What are they going to reschedule?

A second meaning: prediction from present evidence

Be going to can also predict a result when the speaker sees evidence now. This is not the central planning use in Unit 6, but it explains common sentences students will hear.

Olivia is going to be exhausted. Her current agenda shows almost no rest time.
The interview is going to start late. The guests are still traveling to the studio.

What be going to does and does not say

SentenceWhat it clearly saysWhat it does not automatically prove
Olivia is going to rest on Sunday.Resting is her intention.It does not prove that Sunday is blocked in a shared calendar.
Marcus is going to call the studio.Marcus has decided to call.It does not tell us the exact time of the call.
They are going to change the agenda.Changing the agenda is the plan.It does not show which appointments are already confirmed.

Common mistake: missing be

Incorrect: Olivia going to cancel the interview.
Correct: Olivia is going to cancel the interview.
Why: Going to needs the correct form of be.

Common mistake: -ing after going to

Incorrect: She is going to traveling in June.
Correct: She is going to travel in June.
Why: Use the base verb after going to.

Common mistake: double future

Incorrect: She will going to rest.
Correct: She is going to rest. / She will rest.
Why: Do not combine will and going to in one future verb phrase.
Professional audio model: be going to Notice the form of be and the base verb in affirmative, negative, and question examples.
Grammar 2

Present continuous for future arrangements

A confirmed future event usually has evidence: a time, place, person, booking, or shared agreement.

+

Core meaning

The present continuous can describe an action happening now, but with a future time expression it commonly describes a personal arrangement.

subject + am / is / are + verb-ing + future detail
Olivia is meeting the producer on Tuesday at ten. The person, day, and time are known.
They are rehearsing at Studio B tomorrow afternoon. The place and time are organized.
Marcus is not traveling with the band next week. This negative arrangement is already understood.

Evidence of an arrangement

  • A specific person is involved: meeting the producer.
  • A specific time is agreed: at ten thirty.
  • A place or booking exists: at Studio B.
  • Other people know about it: the band has confirmed.

A future expression such as tomorrow, on Friday, or next week prevents confusion with an action happening now.

personwith the producer

Another participant is already involved.

timeat 10:00

The event has a concrete hour.

placeat Studio B

The location is known or booked.

agreementconfirmed by email

Someone else knows and expects it.

Affirmative arrangement

subject + be + verb-ing + future detail
Olivia is meeting the producer on Tuesday.

Negative arrangement

subject + be + not + verb-ing + future detail
Marcus is not traveling with the band next week.

Yes / no question form

be + subject + verb-ing + future detail?
Are they rehearsing on Friday?

Information question form

question word + be + subject + verb-ing?
Where is Olivia recording tomorrow?
General intention

Olivia is going to rehearse more.

The idea exists, but the sentence does not identify a particular rehearsal.

Confirmed arrangement

Olivia is rehearsing with the band at four.

The specific rehearsal has a time and participants.

Action now

Olivia is rehearsing right now.

The time expression changes the meaning from future arrangement to action in progress.

Common mistake: missing be

Incorrect: I meeting Marcus tomorrow.
Correct: I am meeting Marcus tomorrow.
Why: The present continuous always needs be plus the -ing form.

Common mistake: base verb

Incorrect: She is meet the producer on Tuesday.
Correct: She is meeting the producer on Tuesday.
Why: Use the -ing form after am, is, or are.

Common mistake: no future context

Unclear alone: She is recording.
Clear arrangement: She is recording tomorrow at noon.
Why: A future detail makes the intended time clear.
Grammar contrast

How to choose the future form

Start with meaning and evidence, not with the word tomorrow.

+
Step 1Is it an existing intention?

Use be going to: She is going to reduce her workload.

Step 2Are concrete details arranged?

Use the present continuous: She is meeting Marcus at nine.

Step 3Is it an official timetable?

Use the simple present: The train leaves at six.

Step 4Is the decision happening now?

Use will: I will send the message now.

FormSpeaker's viewpointTypical evidenceExample
be going toAn intention or prior plan exists.A personal decision, goal, or plan.Olivia is going to take Sunday off.
present continuousThe event is arranged with practical details.Time, place, booking, or another participant.Olivia is having dinner with her mother on Sunday.
simple presentA timetable controls the event.Transport, class, program, business hours.The concert starts at eight.
willThe decision, offer, or promise is made now.A reaction during the conversation.I will confirm the studio now.

Same event, different stage

We are going to organize an interview. The intention exists.
We are interviewing Olivia at ten on Friday. The arrangement is confirmed.

Decision during the conversation

The photographer is free on Friday. I will book the session now.
Olivia looks exhausted. I will shorten today's meeting.

Common mistake

Incorrect: The train is going to leave at 6:05 according to the official timetable.
Natural timetable form: The train leaves at 6:05.
Why: Official schedules commonly use the simple present.

Future time clauses

Incorrect: Once Marcus will confirm the photo session, they will update the agenda.
Correct: Once Marcus confirms the photo session, they will update the agenda.
Why: After when, once, before, and as soon as, use a present form for the future time clause.
Functional language

Scheduling, conflicts and availability

Good plans need precise verbs, time prepositions, and polite ways to negotiate availability.

+

Four essential scheduling actions

schedule: arrange a new time. Marcus scheduled the interview for Friday.
confirm: make the arrangement certain. The studio confirmed the recording session.
reschedule: move an existing event to another time. They rescheduled the photo session.
cancel: decide that an event will not happen. Olivia canceled the optional meeting.

Look for practical limits

An agenda must include travel, meals, preparation, and rest, not only the visible appointment.

Ask

Are you available on Wednesday morning?

Available means free and able to participate.

Accept

Wednesday morning works for me.

Works for me means the proposed time is suitable.

Reject

Tuesday afternoon does not work for me.

This rejects the time without rejecting the person or the event.

Conflict

The appointment clashes with the interview.

Clashes with means the events overlap or cannot both happen comfortably.

Time prepositions

on

Use with days and dates: on Monday, on July 18, on Friday morning.

at

Use with clock times and precise points: at ten, at noon, at midnight.

in

Use with months, years, and broad periods: in August, in 2027, in the afternoon.

from ... to

Use for a time range: from two to four, from Monday to Wednesday.

Common mistake

Incorrect: The meeting is at Monday.
Correct: The meeting is on Monday.
Why: Use on with days.

Common mistake

Incorrect: We changed the meeting for another hour yesterday.
Clear scheduling verb: We rescheduled the meeting yesterday.
Why: Reschedule states the action precisely.

Useful distinction

postpone: move an event to a later time.
reschedule: assign a different time, which may be earlier or later.

Grammar 3

Advice, options and necessity

Could, should, ought to, and have to do not communicate the same degree of strength.

+

Choose the strength carefully

Could opens a possibility. Should and ought to recommend a good course of action. Have to communicates necessity caused by a rule, requirement, or unavoidable situation.

Advice sounds more useful when it includes a reason: You should move the interview because Thursday has no travel time.

Advice is not an order

Marcus presents options and reasons. Olivia evaluates the trade-offs and remains responsible for her decision.

could

One possible option. It leaves the choice very open.

should

A clear recommendation based on the speaker's judgment.

ought to

Similar to should, sometimes slightly more formal or duty-focused.

have to

A necessity or requirement, not simply ordinary advice.

Soft option

You could move the interview to Friday. This gives Olivia a possible route without pressure.

Clear recommendation

You should protect one evening this week. Marcus thinks this is the best decision.

Serious recommendation

You ought to confirm the medical appointment first. The advice sounds more responsible or duty-focused.

External necessity

You have to leave by five. The schedule, distance, or rule creates the necessity.

Could

subject + could + base verb
Olivia could move the interview.
They could leave more travel time.

Should

subject + should / should not + base verb
She should protect one free evening.
She should not skip lunch.

Ought to

subject + ought to + base verb
She ought to confirm the change today.
Marcus ought to include travel time.

Have to

subject + have / has to + base verb
Olivia has to attend the medical appointment.
They have to leave by five.

Asking for advice

What should + subject + base verb?
What should Olivia move?
Should she confirm Friday first?

Negative recommendation

Use should not or ought not to when the action is a bad idea.

She should not skip the appointment.
She ought not to accept another interview.

No necessity

Does not have to means an action is not necessary. It does not mean the action is prohibited.

Olivia does not have to attend the optional meeting.
Marcus does not have to cancel it; he can reschedule it.

Common mistake: should to

Incorrect: She should to rest.
Correct: She should rest.
Why: Use the base verb directly after should.

Common mistake: missing to

Incorrect: She ought rest.
Correct: She ought to rest.
Why: Unlike should, ought is followed by to.

Common mistake: could to

Incorrect: They could to reschedule it.
Correct: They could reschedule it.
Why: Use the base verb directly after could.

Common mistake: advice noun

Incorrect: Marcus gave Olivia an advice.
Correct: Marcus gave Olivia some advice. / He gave her a piece of advice.
Why: Advice is uncountable in English.

Communication

Responding to advice and making a decision

A natural response can accept the idea, express a limitation, or propose an alternative.

+
Accept

That might work.

The speaker sees the suggestion as possible but has not necessarily made the final decision.

Recognize

That's a good point.

The speaker acknowledges that the reason or concern is valid.

Adopt

I could do that.

The speaker accepts the proposed action as a realistic option.

Preference

I'd rather keep the rehearsal.

The speaker states a preferred alternative without attacking the advice.

Limitation

I'm not sure that would work because...

The speaker disagrees politely and immediately explains the practical reason.

Alternative

What if we moved the interview instead?

The speaker keeps the conversation constructive by proposing another route.

ProblemName the conflict

The interview clashes with the recording session.

AdviceOffer a reasoned option

You could move the interview to Friday morning.

LimitationExplain the trade-off

That might work, but the photo session is not confirmed.

DecisionState the next action

We will confirm the photo session before changing the interview.

Useful response bank

Accepting

That might work. / That's a good point. / I could do that.

Explaining a limit

I'm not sure that would work because the studio is already booked.

Offering another route

What if we moved the interview instead? / Could we keep Friday open?

Finalizing

Let's confirm the photo session first. / I'll send the updated schedule now.

Natural English

Phrasal verbs and idioms for plans and workload

Six carefully selected expressions make scheduling and advice sound more natural.

+

How these expressions fit the unit

They describe adding or removing commitments, postponing plans, uncertainty, shared understanding, and a heavy workload. The Spanish meaning is an approximation; the English explanation and examples show the real use.

Register: these expressions are natural in everyday and professional conversation. In very formal writing, clearer alternatives such as postpone, make available, or reach a shared understanding may be preferable.

Visual meaning bank

The six scenes represent making space, freeing time, moving a plan, uncertainty, agreement, and a heavy workload.

phrasal verbhacer un espacio / lograr incluir

fit in

Meaning: to find enough time or space for something in a busy schedule.

Use it when: an event is possible only if a small opening exists.

Not the same as: simply liking an activity. The expression is about schedule space.

  • Marcus can fit in a short phone call before lunch.
  • Olivia cannot fit another rehearsal in on Thursday.
Pronunciation and examples
phrasal verbliberar tiempo / dejar disponible

free up

Meaning: to make time or resources available by changing or removing another commitment.

Use it when: one change creates useful space in the schedule.

Not the same as: being free all day. It usually means you create availability.

  • Canceling the optional meeting will free up the afternoon.
  • Marcus is trying to free up one evening for Olivia.
Pronunciation and examples
phrasal verbposponer / dejar para despues

put off

Meaning: to postpone something until a later time or date.

Use it when: an event will still happen, but not at the original time.

Common nuance: it can sound neutral, but it can also suggest delaying something that should happen soon.

  • They decided to put off the photo session until Friday.
  • Olivia should not put off her medical appointment.
Pronunciation and examples
idiomestar en el aire / estar sin decidir

up in the air

Meaning: uncertain, undecided, or not completely arranged.

Use it when: an important detail still needs confirmation.

Natural context: plans, dates, locations, budgets, or decisions can be up in the air.

  • The location of Saturday's interview is still up in the air.
  • The travel plans were up in the air until the hotel confirmed.
Pronunciation and examples
idiomestar de acuerdo / compartir la misma idea

on the same page

Meaning: to share the same understanding about a plan, situation, or goal.

Use it when: several people need consistent information before acting.

Natural context: teams, families, classmates, and work partners use it before making a shared decision.

  • Olivia and Marcus need to be on the same page before the tour.
  • Let's review the agenda so everyone is on the same page.
Pronunciation and examples
idiomtener muchas responsabilidades

have a lot on your plate

Meaning: to have many responsibilities, tasks, or problems to manage at the same time.

Use it when: someone's workload explains why another commitment may be unrealistic.

Grammar note: change the possessive: on my plate, on your plate, on her plate, on their plate.

  • Olivia has a lot on her plate this month.
  • Marcus offered to help because she already had too much on her plate.

Integrated explanation

A complete problem-to-decision model

The final conversation combines future plans, arrangements, advice, limitations, and natural expressions.

+

Why this model is effective

The speakers do not jump directly from a problem to an order. They identify the conflict, offer an option, explain a limitation, protect a priority, and agree on the next concrete action.

arrangementintentioncouldshouldought tohave topreferencefinal action

A realistic result

The final agenda is not empty. It is balanced, confirmed, and clear enough for everyone involved.

Professional two-voice model: Olivia and Marcus The visible conversation is teaching input, so students can connect each form to its communicative purpose.
MarcusYour schedule is too crowded next Thursday. You are meeting the producer at ten, recording at noon, and attending a radio interview at two thirty.
OliviaI know. I am also going to visit my mother that evening, so I cannot stay at the studio late.
MarcusYou could put off the radio interview until Friday morning. That would free up enough time for lunch and travel.
OliviaThat might work, but Friday's photo session is still up in the air.
MarcusThen we should confirm the photo session first. If it stays in the afternoon, we can fit in the interview at ten.
OliviaThat is a good point. I would rather keep Thursday focused on recording.
MarcusAgreed. I will call the photographer now, and you ought to tell the producer that you have to leave by five.
OliviaPerfect. Once those details are confirmed, we will send the final agenda to the band so everyone is on the same page.

Three arrangements compete for time and travel.

OptionMove the interview

Trade-offFriday is not fully confirmed
