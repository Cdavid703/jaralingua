The Letter I Never Sent

Lina found the letter inside an old notebook while she was cleaning her desk. The envelope had her cousin's name on it, but there was no stamp. She had written it three years earlier, during the week before she moved to another city for a design program.

In the letter, Lina sounded completely sure about her future. She wrote, "I hope I become a designer in a big company. I wish I could leave this small town quickly and start a life that feels important." At that time, she used to believe that a dream had to look impressive from the outside.

The first months were exciting, but they were also lonely. Lina liked her classes, yet she missed the people who knew her before she had a plan. She used to call her cousin every Friday night, but she stopped when the program became more demanding. Little by little, her dream started to feel heavy.

Then something unusual happened. A teacher asked the class to create posters for a community radio station. Lina interviewed older neighbors, recorded their memories, and designed simple posters for their stories. For the first time, design did not feel like a competition. It felt like a bridge between people.

After that project, Lina did not send the letter. She kept it because it reminded her of the person she used to be: ambitious, nervous, and a little impatient. She did not hate that version of herself. She just understood her better.

Now Lina still hopes to work as a designer, but her dream is different. She wants to design projects that help communities explain who they are. She wishes she had known earlier that changing a dream is not the same as losing it. Sometimes the best dream is not the one that impresses everyone. It is the one that lets you recognize yourself.

"Changing a dream is not the same as losing it." Use this idea to explain the story in your own words after the quiz.
