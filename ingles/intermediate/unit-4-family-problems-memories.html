Course guide

What Unit 4 is really about

The official document has two connected lessons: family problems and demands, then family memories.

+

Central idea

A family can talk about rules, responsibilities, and memories with language that stays clear, respectful, and specific.

This unit does not ask students to expose private family problems. We use a fictional family so students can study the language safely: describe a problem, explain a demand, set a limit, remember a past habit, and propose a fair solution.

The Rivera family scenario

Students need a concrete family before grammar rules make sense.

+
AnaShe organizes dinner. She wants everyone to help for twenty minutes.
CarlosHe wants phones away during dinner, but he also has a work message.
MayaShe has to finish a presentation. She wants Leo to set the table.
LeoHe wants fair rules. He says adults should follow the phone rule too.
RosaShe remembers that the family used to cook together on Sundays.

Problem now

Everyone is busy, but dinner still needs preparation. One person should not do all the work.

Memory before

The family used to protect one hour for Sunday dinner, even when the day was difficult.

Solution goal

The family needs a short, realistic agreement: shared tasks and one phone-free moment.

Grammar 1

Obligation with have to

Use have to when a person has a responsibility or necessary action.

+

Meaning

Have to means the action is necessary because of a rule, responsibility, plan, or situation.

subject + have/has to + base verb
Leo has to set the table. It is his responsibility tonight.
Maya has to finish her presentation. It is necessary for school.
Carlos has to wash the dishes. It is part of the agreement.
Professional audio modelObligation with have to.

Negative form

Don't have to means there is no obligation. It does not mean prohibition.

Ana doesn't have to cook everything alone.

Common mistake

Incorrect: Maya has to finishes her presentation.
Correct: Maya has to finish her presentation.

More classroom models

Parents have to listen before they decide.
Children have to explain their plans clearly.
A family rule has to be fair for everyone.
Grammar 2

Limits with won't let

Use won't let to explain that someone does not allow an action.

+

Meaning

Won't let expresses a limit, rule, or permission that is not given. In this unit, we use it for respectful rules, not aggressive control.

person + won't let + someone + base verb
Carlos won't let Leo use his phone during dinner.
Ana won't let one person do all the work.
The family won't let phones interrupt the first thirty minutes.
Professional audio modelLimits with won't let.

Use this image for

Phone rule, dinner agreement, and respectful negotiation.

Important contrast

Won't let tells us who blocks or limits an action. Can't only says the action is not possible.

Common mistake

Incorrect: Carlos won't let Leo to use his phone.
Correct: Carlos won't let Leo use his phone.

More classroom models

My parents won't let me stay out late on school nights.
The teacher won't let students submit work without names.
A mediator won't let one person interrupt all the time.
Grammar 3

Expectations with want someone to

Use this structure when one person expects or asks another person to do something.

+

Meaning

Want someone to connects a person with the action we expect from them.

person + want(s) + someone + to + base verb
Ana wants everyone to help.
Maya wants Leo to set the table.
Leo wants Maya to help him with English homework.
Professional audio modelExpectations with want someone to.

Arrow thinking

Think of this structure as an arrow:

Ana wants Leo to set the table.
Leo wants Maya to help him tomorrow.

The person after wants is the person who should do the action.

Common mistake

Incorrect: Ana wants that Leo helps.
Correct: Ana wants Leo to help.

Polite upgrade

In mediation, soften demands with phrases like Can you...?, Could you...?, or What if...?

More classroom models

Parents want their children to be responsible.
Teenagers want adults to respect their time.
The mediator wants both sides to explain one need.
Grammar 4

Past habits with used to

Use used to to describe repeated past habits that are different now.

+

Meaning

Used to describes a repeated past action or past state. The important idea is contrast: things were like that before, but now they are different.

subject + used to + base verb
The Riveras used to cook together on Sundays.
Rosa used to tell stories before dinner.
They didn't use to order food very often.

Past

They used to cook together.

Present

Now everyone is busier.

Solution

They will protect thirty minutes for dinner.

Negative and yes/no form

After didn't or did, use use to, not used to.

They didn't use to order food often.
Did they use to eat together every Sunday?

Common mistake

Incorrect: They didn't used to cook together.
Correct: They didn't use to cook together.

More classroom models

My family used to visit my grandmother every Sunday.
We didn't use to eat dinner so late.
Did your family use to celebrate birthdays at home?
Natural English

Phrasal verbs and idioms for family negotiation

Use these expressions to make demands, memories, and solutions sound more natural without losing clarity.

+

Why this belongs in Unit 4

Family conversations often need more than grammar. Students also need natural expressions for raising a problem, helping, reducing tension, agreeing on a solution, and repairing a conflict.

These expressions are not extra decoration. They connect directly with the unit product: a respectful family agreement and a memory reflection.

Register guide

Most expressions below are informal or semi-formal. They are appropriate for family conversations, role-plays, mediation, and personal writing. For formal writing, students should use clearer academic verbs such as mention, resolve, contribute, or compromise.

Phrasal verbs

bring upmention a problem

Ana wants to bring up the phone rule. Use it when someone starts talking about a sensitive topic.

work outsolve

The family needs to work out a fair dinner plan. Use it for finding a solution after discussion.

pitch inhelp

Everyone has to pitch in for twenty minutes. Use it when several people share a task.

set asidereserve

They used to set aside one hour for Sunday dinner. Use it for protecting time, money, or attention for something important.

calm downbecome calmer

Leo needs to calm down before he explains his point. Use it carefully: it can sound rude if said directly during an argument.

Idioms and fixed expressions

clear the airreduce tension

The family needs to clear the air before dinner. Use it when people talk honestly so a tense situation feels better.

meet someone halfwaycompromise

Ana and Leo can meet each other halfway. Use it when both sides give a little to reach a fair agreement.

patch things uprepair a relationship

After the argument, they want to patch things up. Use it when people try to improve a relationship after conflict.

on the same pageshared understanding

Before they start cooking, everyone should be on the same page. This expression already belongs to the intermediate idiom bank and fits Unit 4 mediation.

the ball is in your courtyour turn to act

I explained my responsibility. Now the ball is in your court. This expression already belongs to the intermediate idiom bank and works for family decisions.

Common mistake

Incorrect: The family needs to work out the problem it.
Correct: The family needs to work out the problem. / The family needs to work it out.

Common mistake

Incorrect: Everyone has to pitch the dinner.
Correct: Everyone has to pitch in with dinner.

Common mistake

Incorrect: We need to meet halfway the problem.
Correct: We need to meet halfway. / We need to meet each other halfway.

Communication

Respectful mediation language

Students need language for solutions before they debate or role-play.

+

Conversation path

  1. Recognize: I understand your point.
  2. Explain: I have to finish my homework.
  3. Set a limit: We can't let one person do everything.
  4. Propose: What if everyone helps for twenty minutes?
  5. Agree: Let's agree on one rule everyone can follow.
