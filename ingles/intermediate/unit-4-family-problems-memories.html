Obligations, Limits and Family Memories

Use a fictional family story to talk about responsibilities, permission, respectful disagreement, and past habits with have to, won't let, want someone to, and used to.

The Rivera Family Scenario

The Rivera family is preparing a Sunday dinner. Everyone has a different responsibility, but the family also has homework, work calls, music practice, and old traditions. The goal is not to judge a real family. The goal is to practice useful English with a safe fictional situation.

AnaShe organizes dinner and wants everyone to help for twenty minutes.
CarlosHe wants phones away during dinner, but he has a late work message.
MayaShe has to finish a school presentation and wants Leo to set the table.
LeoHe wants more freedom and says the rules are not always fair.
RosaShe remembers what family dinners used to be like.

Noticing

Core Language

FunctionPatternModel sentence
Obligationsubject + have/has to + base verbMaya has to finish her presentation before Monday.
No obligationsubject + don't/doesn't have to + base verbAna doesn't have to cook everything alone.
Permission or limitssubject + won't let + person + base verbCarlos won't let Leo use his phone during dinner.
Request or expectationsubject + want(s) + person + to + base verbAna wants everyone to help for twenty minutes.
Past habitsubject + used to + base verbThey used to cook together on Sundays.
03

Practice Route

How Unit 4 Works

  1. Start with the grammar practice to build accurate sentences about obligations, permission, and requests.
  2. Listen to the Sunday dinner negotiation and identify responsibilities, limits, feelings, and the final agreement.
  3. Read The Memory Box to notice how used to contrasts past habits with present routines.
  4. Practice pronunciation in short family-story sections before recording the final paragraph.
  5. Complete a mediation role play and write a short family memory blog as the final product.
