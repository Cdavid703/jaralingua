Listening map

Five kinds of evidence to track

A strong listener does not try to remember every word. Organize the information into these five evidence categories while the speakers compare foods.

Main ideaIdentify the program's purpose and the conclusion that connects all examples.
Factual detailTrack time references, countries, dish names, and exact ingredients.
QuantitiesNotice which expressions describe countable items, uncountable ingredients, and portions.
Sensory evidenceListen for contrasts in taste and texture, especially outside versus inside.
Food contextConnect each dish with breakfast, daily life, parties, traditions, or celebrations.

Professional audio bank

Quantity language for a food review

Every play button uses an existing professional ElevenLabs recording. Listen for the complete phrase, not an isolated translation.

Choose a phrase to hear its American English model.

some grapes
affirmative quantity
Use some for an unspecified positive quantity.
There are some grapes for dessert.
not much cereal
uncountable noun
Use much with an uncountable noun, especially in negatives and questions.
There isn't much cereal left.
a few tomatoes
countable plural
Use a few for a small but useful number of countable items.
A few tomatoes can add color and freshness to the dish.
a little oil
uncountable amount
Use a little for a small but sufficient amount of an uncountable ingredient.
The recipe needs a little oil, not a full cup.
a cup of rice
measure + of
A measure makes an uncountable ingredient countable and precise.
A cup of rice is enough for this small recipe.
three cups of cooked rice
plural measure
Pluralize the measure, not the uncountable food noun after of.
The family prepares three cups of cooked rice for everyone.
a slice of bread
partitive
A partitive describes one separated portion of a larger or uncountable food.
Serve the soup with a slice of bread.
a bottle of water
container + of
A container identifies both the amount and the form in which something is served.
A bottle of water is easier to carry than a large pitcher.

Annotated model

How a complete food review is built

This model uses a different dish from the simulation. The colors show the purpose of each sentence so you can reproduce the structure with your own ideas.

Onigiri is a traditional Japanese rice snack that people often eat at lunch or take on trips. It is made with some cooked rice, a little salt, and a small piece of salmon or another filling. There are not many ingredients, but every one has a clear purpose. The rice is soft and slightly sticky, while the seaweed outside is crisp and salty. Compared with a sandwich, onigiri feels lighter and is easier to carry. I first tried it at a Japanese food festival, and I liked the contrast between the warm rice and savory center. I would give it four and a half out of five. I recommend it as an everyday snack because it is simple, filling, and convenient.
Origin and contextName the dish, place, and eating situation.
Ingredients and quantitiesUse precise quantifiers, measures, or partitives.
Sensory evidenceDescribe taste and texture with a clear contrast.
Comparison and experienceConnect the dish with another food and a personal moment.
Rating and recommendationFinish with a justified evaluation.
