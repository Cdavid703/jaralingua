# Intermediate English Course 1 Listening Scripts

## The Mirror Guardian
File: `the-mirror-guardian-listening.mp3`

Narrator: After a big storm, Silver City was full of rumors. People looked at others quickly and gave them simple labels. That was the moment when the Mirror Guardian arrived. He was not the strongest superhero in the city, but he had a rare power. His silver shield could show the truth behind a stereotype.

Neighbor: Dr. Lina Frost never smiles. Maybe she is cold and unfriendly.

Mirror Guardian: Look again. A quiet person is not always an unfriendly person.

Dr. Lina Frost: I work late because families need clean water. These filters are free for them.

Narrator: In the mirror, the neighbors saw Lina building water filters at night. She was serious, but she was also generous and brave.

Maya: People also judge me because of my purple hair, bright boots, and star jacket.

Neighbor: Some adults say you are careless.

Maya: My clothes help me feel brave when I feel shy. And today I helped younger students cross flooded streets.

Narrator: Then they met Mr. Bruno, a mechanic with strong arms and oil on his hands. Some people thought he only cared about engines.

Mr. Bruno: I repair engines, yes, but I also read poetry every night. I repair old wheelchairs for free, too.

Mirror Guardian: One job, one style, or one face cannot tell a full story.

Narrator: Suddenly, the old bridge started to break.

Dr. Lina Frost: The left side is weak. We need to move people away now.

Maya: I can send clear warnings online. People will see them quickly.

Mr. Bruno: I have tools in my truck. I can hold the metal support for a few minutes.

Narrator: Lina checked the structure, Maya sent warnings, and Bruno used his tools. The city was safe.

Mirror Guardian: A stereotype is not a full story. Look again. People are always more than one label.

## Catching Up After Years
File: `catching-up-after-years.mp3`

Maya: Ethan? Wow, I almost walked past you. It has been such a long time.

Ethan: Maya! I know. We have sent short messages, but we have not really talked for years. I am glad we finally chose a day to catch up.

Maya: Me too. So, my timeline is a little messy, but I will try to put it in order. First, after graduation, I moved to Denver for a marketing internship. I was excited, but during the first month I felt really lonely.

Ethan: That sounds hard. What helped you adjust?

Maya: After that, I joined a small design club after work. At first, I only went because I needed friends. Later, I realized I loved creative projects. Then, the company closed suddenly, and I had to move back home.

Ethan: What changed after the company closed?

Maya: A lot. I helped my mom improve her online store. By the time we finished the new product photos and descriptions, her sales were better, and I felt more confident. Recently, I got a job at a community center. I interview neighbors about their stories and memories, so now marketing feels more human to me.

Ethan: That makes sense. Hearing the timeline helps me understand why each change mattered.

Maya: What about you? What has happened in your life?

Ethan: First, my father got sick last year, so I postponed one college semester. I spent many long days at my family's restaurant. At first, I thought I was falling behind everyone else.

Maya: I can imagine. Did that feeling change?

Ethan: Yes. Later, I managed deliveries, created a better schedule, and listened to customers more carefully. Eventually, I realized the semester was not just lost time. It taught me responsibility and organization.

Maya: That is a big lesson.

Ethan: Since then, I returned to classes in January. I am studying business again, but I understand it differently now. Recently, I also started training for a ten-kilometer race because I wanted a goal outside work and school.

Maya: I like that. A good catch-up conversation should not be a competition about who is more successful. It should help us understand the story behind each change.

Ethan: Exactly. Let us not wait years again. Next month, we can meet here and share one new goal.

Maya: Deal. And next time, I want to hear how the race training is going.
