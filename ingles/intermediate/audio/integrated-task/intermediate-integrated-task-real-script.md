# Intermediate Course 1 Integrated Task - Campus Food Fair

File: `intermediate-integrated-task-real-us.mp3`

## Script

Claire: Welcome to Campus Food Fair. Today, Devon and Jordan are choosing three international snacks for our student event. We need clear descriptions, sensible portions, and food that is easy to share. Devon, what did you bring?

Devon: I brought manakish, a Lebanese flatbread. I bought it at a neighborhood bakery last Saturday. The dough uses flour, water, yeast, and a little olive oil. Mine has za'atar on top. The edges are crisp, but the center is soft.

Jordan: How would you serve it at the fair?

Devon: I would cut each flatbread into a few slices. It works as an everyday breakfast, but sharing small pieces makes it practical for an event.

Claire: Jordan, tell us about the hotteok.

Jordan: Hotteok is a Korean sweet pancake. Inside, there is brown sugar, cinnamon, and a few crushed nuts. Use only a little filling, or it can spill while the pancake cooks. It is warm, chewy, and popular as a street snack in cold weather.

Claire: Our third choice is Nigerian puff-puff.

Devon: These small fried dough balls are made with flour, yeast, some sugar, and a little nutmeg. We have many pieces, so I would serve five in each paper cup.

Jordan: Puff-puff is golden outside and light inside. It is sweeter than manakish, and people enjoy it as both an everyday treat and celebration food.

Claire: Excellent. A useful food review connects ingredients, quantities, texture, and cultural context. That evidence helps readers understand both the food and the right portion.
