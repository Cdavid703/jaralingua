# Unit 6 Explanation Audio Scripts

Production notes: professional American English voices, natural pacing, no spoken role labels, no sound effects, and no background music. These clips teach language; they are not assessment audio.

## Unit overview
File: `unit-6-explanation/unit-6-overview.mp3`

Narrator: Unit Six connects three practical skills: making future plans, arranging specific events, and giving useful advice. Olivia Reed is preparing for a very busy month. She has a recording session, two rehearsals, a family event, several interviews, and an important medical appointment. Her coordinator, Marcus, helps her distinguish intentions from confirmed arrangements, identify schedule conflicts, and choose realistic solutions. The goal is not to fill every hour. The goal is to communicate plans clearly, evaluate priorities, and organize time responsibly.

## Future form contrast
File: `unit-6-explanation/future-form-contrast.mp3`

Narrator: English uses different future forms because not every future idea has the same meaning. Olivia is going to record a new song expresses an intention or plan. Olivia is meeting the producer on Tuesday at ten expresses a confirmed arrangement with a specific detail. The studio opens at nine expresses an official schedule. I will call the studio now expresses a decision made at the moment of speaking. The time is future in every sentence, but the speaker's perspective is different.

## Going to model
File: `unit-6-explanation/going-to-model.mp3`

Narrator: Use be going to for an intention or a plan that already exists. Olivia is going to rest after the tour. She is not going to accept another interview on Friday. Is she going to travel in June? Remember that the verb be changes with the subject, but the main verb stays in its base form. Say she is going to travel, not she going to travel, and not she is going to traveling.

## Scheduling dialogue
File: `unit-6-explanation/scheduling-dialogue.mp3`

Marcus: Are you available on Tuesday afternoon?

Olivia: I am meeting the producer at two, and I am rehearsing with the band at four.

Marcus: Then Tuesday does not work. How about Wednesday morning?

Olivia: Wednesday morning works for me. I am not doing anything before noon.

Marcus: Great. I will confirm the interview for Wednesday at ten thirty.

Olivia: Perfect. Please leave enough travel time before the photo session.

Marcus: Good point. I will schedule a forty-five-minute break between them.

## Scheduling language model
File: `unit-6-explanation/scheduling-language.mp3`

Narrator: Clear scheduling language includes the action, the time, and the status of the plan. The rehearsal is scheduled for Thursday at three. The dentist appointment clashes with the radio interview. We need to reschedule one of them. Friday morning is still available. That time works for Olivia. Use on with days and dates, at with clock times, and in with months, years, and parts of the day.

## Advice scale
File: `unit-6-explanation/advice-scale.mp3`

Narrator: Advice has different levels of strength. Could presents one possible option: Olivia could move the interview. Should gives a clear recommendation: she should protect one free evening. Ought to has a meaning similar to should, but it can sound slightly more formal: she ought to confirm the change today. Have to expresses necessity, not ordinary advice: she has to attend the medical appointment. Stronger language should match a stronger reason.

## Responding to advice
File: `unit-6-explanation/responding-to-advice.mp3`

Olivia: I could move the interview, but the journalist is leaving the city that evening.

Marcus: That is a good point. Maybe you should shorten the rehearsal instead.

Olivia: That might work. I would rather keep the rehearsal, though, because the concert is on Saturday.

Marcus: Then we could move the photo session to Friday morning.

Olivia: I could do that. Let us confirm Friday before we change the rest of the schedule.

## Fit in
File: `unit-6-explanation/expression-fit-in.mp3`

Narrator: Fit in means to find enough time or space for something in a busy schedule. Marcus can fit in a short phone call before lunch. Olivia cannot fit another rehearsal into Thursday afternoon.

## Free up
File: `unit-6-explanation/expression-free-up.mp3`

Narrator: Free up means to make time or resources available by changing or removing another commitment. Canceling the optional meeting will free up the afternoon. Marcus is trying to free up one evening for Olivia to rest.

## Put off
File: `unit-6-explanation/expression-put-off.mp3`

Narrator: Put off means to postpone something until a later time or date. They decided to put off the photo session until Friday. Olivia should not put off her medical appointment.

## Up in the air
File: `unit-6-explanation/expression-up-in-the-air.mp3`

Narrator: If a plan is up in the air, it is still uncertain or undecided. The location of Saturday's interview is still up in the air. Their travel plans were up in the air until the organizer confirmed the hotel.

## On the same page
File: `unit-6-explanation/expression-same-page.mp3`

Narrator: To be on the same page means to share the same understanding about a plan or situation. Olivia and Marcus need to be on the same page before they confirm the tour. Let us review the final agenda so everyone is on the same page.

## A lot on your plate
File: `unit-6-explanation/expression-on-your-plate.mp3`

Narrator: To have a lot on your plate means to have many responsibilities or problems to manage. Olivia has a lot on her plate this month. Marcus offered to handle the travel details because she already had too much on her plate.

## Integrated model conversation
File: `unit-6-explanation/integrated-model-conversation.mp3`

Marcus: Your schedule is too crowded next Thursday. You are meeting the producer at ten, recording at noon, and attending a radio interview at two thirty.

Olivia: I know. I am also going to visit my mother that evening, so I cannot stay at the studio late.

Marcus: You could put off the radio interview until Friday morning. That would free up enough time for lunch and travel.

Olivia: That might work, but Friday's photo session is still up in the air.

Marcus: Then we should confirm the photo session first. If it stays in the afternoon, we can fit in the interview at ten.

Olivia: That is a good point. I would rather keep Thursday focused on recording.

Marcus: Agreed. I will call the photographer now, and you ought to tell the producer that you have to leave by five.

Olivia: Perfect. Once those details are confirmed, we will send the final agenda to the band so everyone is on the same page.
