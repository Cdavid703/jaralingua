01

Making a first connection

What we will learn: Open a conversation, share a little information and leave space for the other person.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

A real purpose

You are meeting a new classmate. Learn enough to introduce each other later: a name, a routine, a preference and something you share.

A conversation has turns

  1. Greet and introduce yourself.
  2. Ask one clear question.
  3. Listen and respond to the answer.
  4. Ask for a little more detail.
  5. Close politely.

Open

Keep the exchange going

Close

A good conversation is more than a list of questions. If someone says “I like cooking,” respond to that idea: “What do you usually cook?” You do not need to share every interest.

02

Names and identities

What we will learn: Use the name someone prefers and confirm exact information when you need it.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Given and family names

First name: Laura. Last name: Torres. Full name: Laura Torres.

People may have more than one given name or family name. Ask what name they prefer to use.

Confirm the spelling

Say the letters clearly. Ask again if you are unsure: Could you repeat that, please? Spelling helps confirm identity; you do not need to recite the whole alphabet.

A preferred name

The story of a name

Where does your name come from? asks about its origin or story.

You can say: My parents like this name. You can also say: I’m not sure. You do not need to know its history.

Use fictional contact details in classroom examples. A first conversation can focus on interests and everyday life.

03

Personal information and routines

What we will learn: Choose useful information about your everyday life and answer with one extra detail.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Identity and origin

be + information

I am a student. I’m from Colombia. My classmate is from Medellín.

Everyday routines

subject + present simple

Add frequency

Use usually, often, sometimes or a time phrase when it adds useful information.

What do you do?

In a first conversation, this often asks about your job or main activity: “I work in a shop.” “I’m a student.”

What do you do in your free time?

This asks about activities you choose outside work or class: “I read and listen to music.” The extra words change the purpose of the question.

A useful answer has a direct response and one detail: “I live in Envigado, near a park.” Avoid turning every answer into a long prepared speech.

04

Asking and extending questions

What we will learn: Choose be or do/does according to the main verb, then ask a question connected to the answer.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Questions with be

Be + subject + information?

Question word + be + subject?
Where are you from? What is your last name?

Yes, I am. / No, I’m not. I’m from Bello.

Questions with other verbs

Do + you + base verb?

Question word + do + you + base verb?
Where do you live? What do you usually cook?

Yes, I do. I usually cook soup.

Talking about a classmate

Does + he/she + base verb?

Does she like music? Yes, she does.

Statement: She likes music.
Question: Does she like music?

Notice the difference
Where are you from? → be
Where do you live? → live

Common corrections
“Where you live?” → Where do you live?
“Are you like music?” → Do you like music?
“Does she likes music?” → Does she like music?

Follow the answer

A: I enjoy cooking.
B: What do you usually cook?
A: Vegetable soup.

Return the turn

A: I usually walk after work. What about you?
B: I prefer reading at home.

Ask for a detail before moving to a different topic.

05

Likes and favorite activities

What we will learn: Say what you enjoy, express a preference and give a clear reason.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

An activity or a thing

like / love / enjoy + noun
I like music. I enjoy group activities.

like / love / enjoy + verb-ing
I like listening to music. I enjoy cooking.

Like and love can also take to + verb; this unit practises the -ing pattern. After enjoy, use -ing, not “to cook”.

A stronger choice

Prefer shows a choice between alternatives. Add the context so the listener understands your preference.

A reason

because + a reason
My classmate likes group activities because she enjoys meeting people.

A preference is not an identity

I don’t like noisy places. = a preference.
I’m quiet. = a description of personality.

Use don’t/doesn’t like for dislikes and am/is/are + adjective for descriptions.

Find a favourite

What is your favourite activity?
My favourite activity is cooking.

What do you like doing after class?
I like listening to music.

06

Personality in context

What we will learn: Describe yourself with an adjective and an example, and listen to how another person describes themselves.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Sometimes uncomfortable when meeting new people.

I’m shy at first, but I enjoy talking in small groups.

Comfortable meeting and talking to people.

I’m outgoing. I often start conversations.

Not talking very much in a particular situation.

I’m quiet in class, but I ask questions when I need help.

Often talking a lot.

I’m talkative when I’m with my friends.

Enjoying movement or doing many things.

I’m active on weekends. I like walking.

Usually relaxed in a situation.

I’m calm when plans change.

Kind and pleasant towards other people.

I’m friendly. I welcome new classmates.

Enjoying new ideas or making things.

I’m creative. I enjoy drawing.

Able to wait or help without becoming annoyed quickly.

I’m patient when I explain things.

Planning things and keeping them in order.

I’m organized. I write my plans in a notebook.

Build a description

be + adjective, then add an example.

Ask instead of guessing

A person who plays video games may be outgoing. A person who plays sports may be quiet.

Quiet and shy are different ideas. Active and outgoing are different ideas. Preferences alone do not define a personality.

Your description may change with the situation. “I’m quiet at work, but I’m talkative with my friends” is a useful, complete answer.

07

Similarities and differences

What we will learn: Report what you share and what is different without needing identical answers.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

A shared interest

both refers to the two people. It comes before the main verb here: We both enjoy walking.

A difference

Use but to connect different preferences. Remember prefers / likes / enjoys with he or she.

A short report

This is my classmate Laura. She is friendly and creative. She works in a bakery. We both like cooking, but we have different routines.

If you have no shared interest yet, ask about another activity. You can also report a real difference: “We like different activities.” Do not invent a similarity just to complete a sentence.

08

Natural social expressions

What we will learn: Distinguish phrasal verbs, idiomatic language and ordinary social phrases, and use them for a real purpose.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Phrasal verb

llevarse bien con

Have a friendly relationship with someone.

Use: Everyday, neutral. Keep with before the person.

Phrasal verb

abrirse o hablar con más confianza

Start talking more openly about your thoughts or feelings.

Use: Conversational. People can take time to open up.

Idiom

romper el hielo

Help people feel more comfortable at the start of an interaction.

Use: Common in social and classroom situations.

Idiomatic expression

tener algo en común

Share an interest, characteristic or experience.

Use: Everyday, neutral. Name the shared interest when possible.

Nice to meet you, That sounds interesting and What about you? are useful social phrases. They are not presented as phrasal verbs. Learn an expression as a complete idea, then notice how it works in the example.

What to notice

  • I’m connects I and am; What’s connects what and is.
  • Stress the main syllable: FRIEND-ly, cre-A-tive, out-GO-ing.
  • Finish the third-person verb: she likes, he enjoys.
  • Pause between ideas. Clear speech matters more than speed.
10

Unit summary

What we will learn: Check what you understand before applying the language with a classmate.

  • I can start and close a conversation.
  • I can confirm a name and ask useful personal questions.
  • I can distinguish questions with be from questions with do/does.
  • I can explain a preference with a reason.
  • I can describe personality with an example.
  • I can ask a follow-up question and listen to the answer.
  • I can report shared interests and introduce my classmate.

Your classroom task is to interview a classmate and introduce each other. Use short notes, listen to your partner and improve one part after feedback. The unit explanation prepares that task.
