01

Leisure Time and Music

Interests, music, object pronouns and abilities.

1.1

Free-time activities

What we will learn: Distinguish interests, hobbies and free time, and describe the activities you choose.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

An interest

A subject or activity you like learning about. You can be interested in music without playing an instrument.

A hobby

An activity you do regularly for enjoyment: drawing, gardening or taking photos.

Free time

Time outside work, study and other responsibilities. You can rest, spend time with others or practise a hobby.

Making pictures with pencils or similar materials.

Taking and creating photographs.

Growing or caring for plants.

Enjoying books, stories or articles.

Creating bracelets, necklaces or other decorative items.

Making music with a guitar.

Inside and outside

Indoor: reading, making jewellery, playing board games.

Outdoor: gardening, cycling, going for a walk.

Some hobbies can happen in either place. Photography is one example.

Alone or with others

These are different ways to spend free time. Explain your own choice with because.

Recover the Unit 1 patterns: What do you do in your free time? and I enjoy + verb-ing. Add a useful detail rather than memorising a long list.

1.2

Music and preferences

What we will learn: Talk about music genres and explain a preference clearly.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Popular music with memorable melodies.

A broad genre often featuring electric guitars and drums.

A broad musical tradition often including improvisation.

A broad tradition including orchestral and chamber music.

A dance music genre with Afro-Caribbean roots.

A popular genre associated with a recognisable rhythmic pattern.

Ask about a genre

I like salsa because I enjoy the rhythm.

Artist, singer, band or song?

An artist creates or performs music. A singer sings. A band is a group of musicians. A song is one piece of music, usually with words.

What is your favourite song?
Who is your favourite singer?

Describe a preference

Musicians can work in more than one genre. A class popularity chart shows the preferences of that group, not a universal ranking.

1.3

Object pronouns

What we will learn: Choose the pronoun for the person or thing receiving an action or following a preposition.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Subject

She plays the guitar.

She tells us who does the action.

Object

I listen to her.

Her refers to the person I listen to. It follows the preposition to.

I → me

you → you

he → him

she → her

it → it

we → us

they → them

After a verb

verb + object pronoun

Help me. Invite them. Join us.

Keep the pronoun in the object position: “My friends invite me,” not “invite I”.

After a preposition

with + him/her/us/them
to + him/her/it/them
for + me/you/us

I play with him. I listen to them. This invitation is for you.

Make the reference clear

Them refers to the musicians in the band. For one song, use it: “This song is great. I like it.”

She and her refer to the same person, but do different jobs. Correct: “I listen to her.” Incorrect: “I listen to she.” An object pronoun replaces a clear noun or noun phrase.

1.4

Abilities with can

What we will learn: Say what someone can do and ask about an ability without adding to or a third-person s.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Affirmative

subject + can + base verb

I can swim. They can draw. The form of can stays the same with every subject.

Negative

subject + cannot / can’t + base verb

Cannot is normally written as one word. Can’t is the contraction.

Questions and short answers

Can + subject + base verb?

What can you make?
I can make bracelets.

Present simple

She plays tennis.
Does she play tennis?

With can

She can play tennis.
Can she play tennis?

Correct: She can play. Avoid “She can plays”, “She can to play” and “Does she can play?” For this section, can expresses ability.

02

Handy Work and Sports

Materials, purpose, sports and actions in context.

2.1

Making things

What we will learn: Name materials, actions and handmade activities before explaining a small project.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Small decorative objects with holes for thread.

A thin strand used in sewing or craft work.

Material from trees used to build or make things.

Material used for drawing, folding or writing.

A coloured substance used to decorate a surface.

A tool used to apply paint.

Activities

Jewellery making: making bracelets or necklaces.

Woodworking: making things from wood. Carpentry is related work that often involves building or fitting wooden structures.

Painting: creating pictures or decorating a surface with paint.

Actions

make a bracelet · build a small boat · paint a picture · cut thread · choose colours · add beads

Handy work is the label in the course guide. In this explanation, crafts and making things describe the hobby context more directly. Learn the action together with its object.

2.2

Purpose and simple instructions

What we will learn: Explain what a material is for and follow a short sequence using action verbs.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Ask about purpose

What for? is a shorter question when the object is already clear.

Explain the purpose

use + material / it + to + base verb
We use wood to build a small boat.

To make tells us the purpose. It does not mean the action is happening now.

First

Choose two colours.

→
Then

Cut a piece of thread.

→
After that

Add the beads.

→
Finally

Tie the ends.

An instruction often begins with the base verb: choose, cut, add, tie. The subject you is understood. These examples illustrate the language of a sequence. The subject you is understood in each instruction.

2.3

Sports and activity patterns

What we will learn: Use familiar sports expressions and ask how two activities are different.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

play + sport or game

Use these complete expressions. Play is common with ball sports and games.

go + activity in -ing

Do not insert to: I go swimming, not “I go to swimming”.

do + activity

These are common combinations. The patterns are useful guides; learn each activity with its usual verb.

Ask about a sport

How do you play this sport?
Explain the players, main action and a simple rule.

Where is it popular?
Use information provided in the lesson or an appropriate source.

Ask about a difference

Use between A and B. A simple answer can name an important feature: “In soccer, players usually use their feet. In basketball, they use their hands.”

For an earnings question, use How much does a soccer player earn? After does, use the base form earn, not earns. Actual earnings vary; the language example does not require memorising a salary.

2.4

Habits and actions in progress

What we will learn: Choose the tense according to time and distinguish an activity name from a continuous verb.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

A repeated activity

Present simple: a regular activity.
She makes jewellery on Saturdays.

Do you make bracelets?
Yes, I do. / No, I don’t.

An action in progress

am/is/are + verb-ing: an action in progress now or around now.

What are you making?
I’m making a necklace.

An activity after enjoy

I enjoy making things.

Here, making names the activity I enjoy. It is not a continuous tense because there is no am/is/are before making.

I’m making something.
Here, am making is the continuous verb.

Compare every weekend with right now. Changing the tense changes the time meaning. This section applies language from Basic 2 rather than presenting a new tense.

03

The Natural World

Places, outdoor activities and useful information.

3.1

Nature and outdoor hobbies

What we will learn: Describe a natural place and connect it to an outdoor activity.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

An area with many trees.

Water flowing along a natural channel.

A body of water surrounded by land.

A path used for walking or other outdoor activities.

An area of land higher than the ground around it.

Water falling from a height.

Outdoor hobbies

Hiking: walking on trails, often in natural areas.
Camping: staying outdoors, usually in a tent.
Birdwatching: observing birds.

Describe a place

Choose a precise detail: quiet, green, rocky, shady, steep.

The trail is shady. There is a river near the forest. I like this place because it is quiet.

Use the information in a map or description. A simple nature description can include one responsible habit: We take our rubbish home. This connects the outdoor topic with care for the environment.

3.2

Useful information questions

What we will learn: Distinguish duration, distance, frequency and price before answering.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Duration

It lasts two hours.

Ask about time, not distance.

Distance

It is two kilometres away.

Ask how distant one place is from another.

Frequency

Twice a month.

Ask how regularly an activity happens.

Price

It costs twenty dollars.

Ask the price of an activity or service.

How much can ask for an amount

How much thread do we need?
We need one metre.

How much does the thread cost?
It costs two dollars.

The first question asks for an amount. The second asks for a price.

Use a base verb after does

How long does it last?
How much does it cost?

Answer: It lasts two hours. It costs twenty dollars.

The times, distances and prices here are fictional teaching examples. They show what kind of information each question requests.

3.3

Location and sequence

What we will learn: Locate a place, organise steps and recognise common contractions.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Where is it?

behind, not “behind of”.
in front of, with of.

The forest is near the lake. The meeting point is to the right of the lake.

When does it happen?

before / after place events in time:
We meet before the walk.
We rest after the activity.

First, then, after that, finally organise steps. Location words and sequence words answer different questions.

Short forms in context

Who’s = who is here.
He’s = he is here.
She’s making a bracelet = she is making a bracelet.

The context tells you which words have been shortened.

04

Hobby Groups

Group descriptions, expressions and complete models.

4.1

Joining and describing a group

What we will learn: Combine interests, abilities and information questions to understand a hobby group.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

What a useful group description includes

  1. What the group enjoys.
  2. What members can do.
  3. When and where they meet.
  4. What they usually do and what they are doing now.
  5. A purpose and an invitation.

Ask before joining

What do you do in the group?
Can beginners join?
How often do you meet?
How long does each meeting last?
How much does it cost?

Connect to the group

Us refers to the members of the group. Use it to connect the invitation with the people already introduced.

The group profile is an explained language model: it connects the activity, the members, the schedule and the invitation.

4.2

Expressions for interests and hobbies

What we will learn: Understand natural multiword expressions and use one in a complete context.

SpeedTap a word or sentence to listen.

Phrasal verb

empezar una afición

Begin an activity as a hobby.

Use: Everyday, neutral. Put the activity after take up.

Phrasal verb

probar para ver cómo funciona

Test an activity, method or piece of equipment.

Use: Everyday, neutral. With a pronoun: try it out.

Phrasal verb

participar

Take part in an activity with other people.

Use: Everyday, neutral. Use join us for the people, or join in for participation.

Idiom

empezar a dominar algo

Learn how to do something through practice.

Use: Informal, common. Follow of with an activity or a noun.

Idiom

no ser lo mío

Say that something is not your preference.

Use: Conversational. It expresses a personal preference.

The Spanish explanation gives an approximate meaning. Learn the English example and context rather than translating the expression word by word.

4.4

Unit summary

What we will learn: Recognise the vocabulary, grammar and communication goals connected by the examples.

  • I can describe my interests and explain a preference.
  • I can use object pronouns with a clear reference.
  • I can say what I can and can’t do.
  • I can ask and answer questions with can.
  • I can describe a hobby and explain a simple purpose.
  • I can distinguish a habit from an action in progress.
  • I can ask about duration, distance, frequency and price.
  • I can describe a natural place and locate its features.
  • I can understand and introduce a hobby group.
