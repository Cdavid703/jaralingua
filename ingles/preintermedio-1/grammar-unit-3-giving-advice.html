Exercise 1 · Advice and suggestions

Complete each gap with the words in brackets and the modal named in the prompt. Write only the missing words. Use should for advice, should not for negative advice, and could for a possible suggestion.

Worked example

I feel stressed. You should ask for help. You could talk to a classmate.

Use the modal named in each prompt. Other suggestions may be possible in a real conversation; this exercise checks the requested form.

Exercise 2 · Required, forbidden or optional?

Complete each gap with a form of must or have to and the verb in brackets. Read whether the situation describes a requirement, something forbidden, or something optional. For the last two questions, use have to as requested.

Worked example

A rule can require an action: you must sign in. An optional action is different: you do not have to bring a printed copy.

Must and have to can both express a requirement. Items 1 and 2 accept both when correctly formed. Must not means forbidden; do not have to means optional. The final two items specifically request questions with have to.

Exercise 3 · If and when: usual responses

Complete each gap with the present simple form of the words in brackets. These if and when sentences describe habitual situations and usual responses. Write only the missing words, including the subject where it appears in brackets.

Worked example

When she feels tired, she takes a break. If I have a busy morning, I make a list.

The connectors if and when are already supplied. These sentences describe usual situations, not future predictions. Complete only the gap.
