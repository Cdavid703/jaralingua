Your grammar goal

Ask about a usual number of hours, offer an activity to try, and describe decided intentions with be going to.

01

Exercise 1 · How many hours?

Complete the gaps with the present simple form of the words in brackets. The questions ask about a usual number of hours. In the two answers, include the given number and the word hours. Write only the missing words.

Worked example

How many hours do you study each week? I study four hours each week.

How many hours asks about time in hours. It is different from how often, which asks about frequency. Items 5 and 6 use fictional answers: the numbers can be written as words or numerals.

Exercise 2 · You could try…

Complete each gap using could try followed by the activity in the ing form. Use the words in brackets, and write only the missing words. Each sentence offers a possible activity to try.

Worked example

You could try taking a short break. She could try making a short list.

Practise could + try + activity in -ing. Keep words such as to in listen to and down in write down. This exercise asks for the suggested activity given in brackets.

03

Exercise 3 · My plans and intentions

Complete each gap with am, is or are going to and the base verb in brackets. For negative sentences, put not before going to. Include the subject where it appears in brackets. These sentences describe intentions that the people have already decided on.

Worked example

I am going to make a list tonight. We are not going to work late. Are you going to take a break?

Use be going to for the stated intentions, including am, is or are. Put not before going to in negatives. After to, use the base verb. Full forms and contractions are accepted where they fit the sentence.
