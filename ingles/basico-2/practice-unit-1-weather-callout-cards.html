Practice Lab · Unit 1 · Activity 01

Weather Call-Out Cards

Look at each card, answer the teacher's question aloud, reveal the model sentence, listen to the professional audio, and make a quick weather decision.

Start activity Back to Practice Lab

Activity type

Practice / Speaking, not grammar

This activity checks recognition and oral production after the Course Overview explanation. The grammar pattern is simple: It's + weather word.

Teacher asks: What's the weather like? Student answers aloud Round 1: identify the weather Round 2: answer a follow-up Round 3: make a decision

Teacher flow

How to run the activity

1. Call a student

Point to a card and ask: What's the weather like? The student responds before the answer is visible.

2. Confirm the model

Click Show answer, then Listen. The class repeats the correct sentence once.

3. Add a decision

Ask a follow-up question such as Can we go out? or What can we do? The student gives one short decision.

Round map

Three quick speaking rounds

Round 1

Weather recognition

Student looks at the image and says: It's cloudy.

Round 2

Teacher follow-up

Teacher asks: Can we go out? Student answers with a short sentence.

Round 3

Weather decision

Student chooses a realistic action: We can stay in. / We can meet up later.

Activity 01 · Round 1

Weather recognition cards

Keep the answer hidden until the student tries to say it.

Card 01

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's sunny.Standard and very common in everyday weather talk.
Card 02

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's cloudy.Standard, clear, and very common for basic weather talk.
Card 03

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's rainy.Correct and natural; It's raining is more common for rain happening now.
Card 04

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's pouring.Natural and colloquial. Use it when the rain is very heavy.
Card 05

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's boiling.Colloquial and more common in British English; in American English, say It's really hot.
Card 06

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's freezing.Very common in American and British English for extremely cold weather.
Card 07

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's windy.Standard and very common. Safe for everyday and classroom English.
Card 08

What's the weather like?

Student says the sentence before opening the answer.

It's stormy.Standard and correct, but less frequent than It's raining.

Activity 01 · Round 2

Teacher follow-up questions

After the weather sentence, choose one quick question. The student answers aloud with a short complete sentence.

Question 1

Can we go out?

Model: Yes, we can go out. / No, we should stay in.

Question 2

Is it good weather for soccer?

Model: Yes, it is. / No, it isn't.

Question 3

Should we stay in?

Model: Yes, we should stay in. / No, we can go out.

Question 4

What can we do?

Model: We can go for a walk. / We can meet up later.

Question 5

What should we bring?

Model: We should bring an umbrella. / We should bring water.

Question 6

Should we call off the game?

Model: Yes, let's call it off. / No, let's play.

Activity 01 · Round 3

Weather decisions

Students connect the weather to a realistic classroom decision. Reveal the model only after the student speaks.

Sunny

It's sunny. What can we do?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: We can go out and get some fresh air.
Cloudy

It's cloudy. Can we still go out?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: Yes, we can go out, but the weather may change.
Rainy

It's rainy. What should we bring?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: We should bring an umbrella.
Pouring

It's pouring. Should we play outside?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: No, let's call off the game.
Boiling

It's boiling. What should we do?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: We should stay in, drink water, and take a break.
Freezing

It's freezing. Can we meet outside?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: We can meet inside or wear warm clothes.
Windy

It's windy. Is it good weather for a picnic?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: Not really. We can meet up later.
Stormy

It's stormy. Should we go out?

Student gives one decision aloud.

Model: No, we should stay in.
