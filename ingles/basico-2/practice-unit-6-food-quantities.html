Quick reminder: a few, some, many, much & a little

Countable: foods you can count (one egg, two eggs). Uncountable: foods we measure as an amount (rice, milk).

A few

A small number. Use it with plural countable nouns.

We need a few tomatoes.

Some

An unspecified number or amount. Use it with plural countable or uncountable nouns, usually in positive sentences, offers and requests.

We have some eggs and some milk.
Would you like some water?

Many

A large number. Use it with plural countable nouns. It is common in questions and negatives.

How many apples do we need?
We don't have many apples.

Much

A large amount. Use it with uncountable nouns, especially in questions and negatives.

How much milk do we need?
There isn't much milk.

A little

A small amount. Use it with uncountable nouns.

Add a little oil.

Remember: a few eggs, but a little rice. Keep a: few and little without it suggest “not many / not much — almost none”. In everyday positive sentences, a lot of is often more natural: “We have a lot of milk.”
