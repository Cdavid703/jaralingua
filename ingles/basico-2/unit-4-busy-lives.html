Course Overview · Unit 4

Busy Lives

Talk about finished activities from last night and last week. Build clear, natural messages about a real and busy life.

Simple pastRecent timeQuestions and answersSound practice

Unit objective

Describe recent, completed actions with confidence.

By the end of this unit, you will be able to say what you did last night or last week, ask another person about a past activity, give a short answer, and organize a short sequence of completed events.

What did you do last night?I cooked dinner.Did you go out?

Learning route

  1. Locate the moment in the past.
  2. Choose the correct past verb.
  3. Build an affirmative sentence.
  4. Ask, answer, and say what did not happen.
  5. Connect events in a short busy-life story.
New language focus: this is the Unit 4 explanation of the simple past. We do not repeat present continuous, shopping demonstratives, or comparative forms here. Use the past when an action started and finished before now.

Open one topic at a time

Unit 4 teaching sequence

Every topic begins with a precise learning purpose and leads toward clear communication about recent life events.

01Locate the past momentUse a time expression to show when a finished action happened.

What we will learn: how to connect one completed action to a clear recent time: last night, yesterday, last week, on Monday, or two days ago.

NOW ← last night ← yesterday ← last week
Last night

I watched a movie last night.

Yesterday

We cooked dinner yesterday.

Last week

She visited her aunt last week.

These expressions tell the listener that the action is complete. They are a helpful signal for choosing the simple past.

02Everyday actions in a busy lifeName familiar home, work, study, and social activities.

What we will learn: the action vocabulary needed to tell a realistic story about one evening or one week.

Home and rest

cook dinner · clean the house · watch a movie · stay home

Work and study

work late · finish a project · study English · answer messages

Social life

visit a friend · go out · rent a movie · talk on the phone

First choose a meaningful action. Then place it in the past: I worked late last night.

03Simple past: affirmative sentencesSay what happened and finished before now.

What we will learn: the basic structure for a completed action. The time expression makes the meaning precise.

Subject + past form of the verb + completed-action detail + time expression.
I cooked dinner last night.

One completed home activity.

We rented a movie yesterday.

One completed shared plan.

My friend visited me last week.

One completed visit.

✓ Past meaning: I studied English last night.
✕ Not for a finished past action: I study English last night.
04Regular verbs: spelling with -edApply the main written changes before pronouncing the ending.

What we will learn: four reliable spelling patterns for regular past verbs, so written sentences look complete and accurate.

Base verbRulePast form
cook / watchAdd -ed.cooked / watched
liveVerb ends in -e: add -d.lived
stopShort stressed vowel + final consonant: double it, then add -ed.stopped
studyConsonant + y: change y to i, then add -ed.studied

Spelling and sound are different ideas: first write the correct form, then notice how -ed sounds.

05Common irregular verbsRecognize high-frequency past forms that do not use -ed.

What we will learn: a small, useful set of past forms for everyday messages. Learn these as pairs.

06Why -ed sounds differentHear /t/, /d/, and /ɪd/ and learn why each ending changes.

What we will learn: regular past verbs always use -ed in writing, but the ending has three pronunciations. The final sound of the base verb decides which one we hear.

Listen to the last sound, not only to the letters. Put two fingers lightly on your throat. A voiced sound vibrates; a voiceless sound does not. English chooses an ending that connects naturally to that final sound.

/t/ Light, voiceless ending

Use /t/ after a voiceless final sound: /p/, /k/, /f/, /s/, /ʃ/, /tʃ/. Your throat does not vibrate, so English adds a light /t/.

Why? Say work. Its last sound is /k/ and your throat does not vibrate. Therefore: worked ends in /t/.

/d/ Voiced ending

Use /d/ after a vowel or voiced final sound: /b/, /g/, /v/, /z/, /l/, /m/, /n/, /r/. Your throat is already vibrating, so the voice continues naturally into /d/.

Why? Say play or clean. You can feel vibration at the end. Therefore: played and cleaned end in /d/.

/ɪd/ Extra-syllable ending

Use /ɪd/ only after the sounds /t/ or /d/. A second /t/ or /d/ would be difficult, so English adds a short extra syllable. This is the only group with an extra syllable.

Why? Say want, then try to add another /t/. It is not smooth. English says want-ed /ˈwɒn.tɪd/ instead.
Important: do not read every written -ed as /ɪd/. worked has one syllable; wanted has an extra syllable. Follow sound, not spelling: laughed ends in /f/, so its -ed sounds /t/.

07Yes/no questions with didAsk politely about a completed activity.

What we will learn: use did first and return the main verb to its base form.

Did + subject + base verb + time expression?
Did you go out last night?

Ask about one completed plan.

Did she work late yesterday?

Ask about completed work.

Did they watch the movie?

Ask about a shared event.

✓ Correct: Did you go to the concert?
✕ Do not use two past forms: Did you went to the concert?
08Short answers and negativesReply clearly and explain what did not happen.

What we will learn: give a direct answer first, then add a simple reason or extra detail when needed.

Yes, I did.

Did you study last night?
Yes, I did. I studied for one hour.

No, I didn't.

Did you go out?
No, I didn't. I stayed home.

Subject + didn't + base verb

We didn't rent a movie.
She didn't work late.

✓ Correct: I didn't go out.
✕ After didn't, do not use past: I didn't went out.
09Tell a short busy-life storyConnect completed events in a logical order.

What we will learn: move from isolated sentences to a coherent message. A mini-story has a past time, several actions, and a closing idea.

First, ...   Then, ...   After that, ...   Finally, ...
Model: A busy Friday
Last Friday was busy. First, I worked late. Then, I cooked dinner. After that, I studied English. Finally, I called it a night.
10Use the past in real-life messagesRecognize the unit language in a diary, voicemail, and WhatsApp reply.

What we will learn: adapt the same simple-past language to different everyday communication purposes.

Diary entry

Record a party or a busy evening: who was there, what happened, and how you felt.

Voicemail

Leave a message about your past week: greeting, two or three actions, closing.

WhatsApp reply

Reply to good news: “I saw your message yesterday. I was very happy.”

Different formats change the audience and tone, but the grammar stays tied to one finished time.

12Unit success checklistKnow what you should understand before moving to Practice Lab.

What we will learn: identify the complete communication goal of the unit, not only one grammar rule.

  • I can use a past-time expression.
  • I can choose a regular or irregular past form.
  • I can write a completed-action sentence.
  • I can ask a question with Did ...?
  • I can answer with Yes, I did or No, I didn't.
  • I can make a negative with didn't + base verb.
  • I can notice common -ed sound patterns.
  • I can tell a short story about a busy night or week.
