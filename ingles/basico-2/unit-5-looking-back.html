Basic English Course 2 · Unit 5

Looking Back

Talk about first experiences, vacation memories, and feelings. The key language is was / were: use it to say where you were, how you felt, and what life was like before now.

MemoriesPast beFeelingsStorytelling
Start with was / wereBack to overview
Unit objective: By the end of this unit, students will be able to recount a simple personal memory or vacation experience, use was / were accurately in affirmative, negative, and question forms, explain a past feeling, and organize a short story in a clear sequence.
01 · RememberSet a past time and place.
02 · DescribeUse was or were for states.
03 · ExplainSay what happened and how you felt.
04 · ConnectTell one short memory in order.
01First experiences and memoriesIntroduce a moment that happened before now.

What we will learn: how to name a personal memory before we explain the details.

My first…

My first trip · my first day at school · my first concert.

The first time…

The first time I saw the ocean was unforgettable.

When I was little…

When I was little, I was very shy.

A memory needs a point in the past. It can be a first experience, a vacation, a childhood moment, or a funny situation.

02Put the memory in the pastShow the listener that the moment is finished.

What we will learn: use time expressions that place a memory clearly before now.

NOW←last summer←two years ago←when I was little
Last summer

I was in Cartagena last summer.

Two years ago

We were on vacation two years ago.

When I was little

I was shy when I was little.

03The past of be: was and wereCompare who you are now with how you were before.

What we will learn: am, is, and are describe a state now. was and were describe a state in the past. This is the central grammar point of Unit 5.

NOW · present

I am calm today.

BEFORE · past

I was nervous on my first flight.

BEFORE · past

My friends were happy at the beach.

Present: now

I→am
he / she / it→is
you / we / they→are
→

Past: before now

I / he / she / it→
you / we / they→
Subject + was / were + place, feeling, or description.

Use was with I, he, she, it. Use were with you, we, they. Tap the exact past form you need to hear; each button plays its own ElevenLabs model.

04Affirmative sentences with was / wereSay where someone was or how someone felt.

What we will learn: build a complete past description with the correct subject and form of be.

I / he / she / it + was   ·   you / we / they + were
I was shy.
She was excited.
We were happy.

Each sentence has a separate professional model. Listen again only to the sentence you want to compare.

05Negative sentences: wasn’t and weren’tExplain what a past state was not.

What we will learn: add not after was or were. The common short forms are wasn’t and weren’t.

Singular or I

I / he / she / it→
+

Plural or you

you / we / they→
I wasn’t nervous.
We weren’t late.
They weren’t at home.

Use weren’t with they.

✓ Correct: We weren’t tired.
✕ Incorrect: We wasn’t tired.
06Questions and short answersAsk about a past place, state, or feeling.

What we will learn: move was or were to the beginning of a yes/no question. Do not use did with the verb be.

Was / Were + subject + complement?
Was your first vacation exciting?

Yes, it was. / No, it wasn’t.

Were you shy when you were little?

Yes, I was. / No, I wasn’t.

Where were you last summer?

I was at my grandmother’s house.

How was the trip?

It was amazing, but the hotel was expensive.

✓ Correct: Were they tired?
✕ Do not add did: Did were they tired?
07Past states versus past actionsChoose was / were for a state and a past verb for an action.

What we will learn: distinguish how someone was from what someone did. Both belong to the past, but they answer different questions.

STATE · How was I?

I was nervous.

ACTION · What did I do?

I went on my first flight.

REACTION · How did I feel?

I felt proud after the trip.

Last summer, I was in Cartagena with my family. We went to the beach every morning. I felt excited because it was my first time near the ocean.
08Feelings and reactionsExplain how a memory made you feel.

What we will learn: use was / were and felt with feeling adjectives in a meaningful past story.

excitednervousshyembarrassedproudsurprised
I was embarrassed.

A past state or feeling.

I felt embarrassed.

A personal reaction to what happened.

It was embarrassing.

Describe the situation, not the person.

Use because to give a simple reason: I felt nervous because I got lost.

09Vacation memory verbsHear useful irregular past forms one by one.

What we will learn: recognize the past forms that help turn a vacation memory into a story. These forms extend Unit 4; they are not a new tense.

Tap each form separately. Do not listen to a long list: compare the one verb you are reading with its own model.

We went to the mountains.

A completed trip.

I took many photos.

A completed vacation action.

I saw the ocean for the first time.

A first experience.

10Useful expressions with go and getUse complete expressions in a memory, not isolated words.

What we will learn: use common past expressions with go and get in a natural context.

go back → went back

volver a un lugar

get home → got home

llegar a casa

get lost → got lost

perderse

Important: learn the complete expression. Got lost is one idea; it does not mean “received lost.”

11Funny and embarrassing storiesTell a safe, respectful anecdote.

What we will learn: describe a small unexpected moment without making fun of another person or exposing private information.

Setting

Where were you? Who was with you?

Moment

What happened?

Reaction

How did you feel, and why?

Model: Last year, I was at a museum with my cousin. We got lost near the exit, but a guard helped us. At first, I felt embarrassed. Then we laughed and went back to the tour.
12Build a clear memory storyMove from notes to a short, connected narrative.

What we will learn: organize a memory with a past time, a state, one or two actions, a feeling, and a closing idea.

1. When / where→2. was / were→3. action→4. feeling
Last summer, I was …   First, I …   Then, I …   I felt … because …

This explanation prepares students for a future blog entry or magazine-style memory; it is not a Practice Lab activity yet.

13Phrasal verbs and idioms for memoriesRecognize the difference before using an expression.

What we will learn: a phrasal verb has a verb plus a particle; an idiom is a fixed expression with a complete meaning.

Phrasal verb: go back

Spanish approximation: volver. “I went back to the beach.”

Phrasal verb: get over → got over

Spanish approximation: superar. “I got over my fear of flying.”

Idiom: a blast from the past

Spanish approximation: algo que trae recuerdos del pasado.

Idiom: a trip down memory lane

Spanish approximation: recordar momentos del pasado con nostalgia.

Idiom: the time of my life

Spanish approximation: pasar un momento inolvidable.

These expressions are also available in the general Idioms and Phrasal Verbs library with their individual audio models.

14Unit 5 success checklistConfirm the language you can use before Practice Lab is planned.

What we will learn: check the complete communication goal, not only one grammar rule.

  • I can describe a first experience or vacation memory.
  • I can choose was or were.
  • I can make a negative with wasn’t or weren’t.
  • I can ask and answer a question with was or were.
  • I can tell the difference between a past state and a past action.
  • I can explain how I felt and give a reason with because.
  • I can use a past expression with go or get.
  • I can tell a short memory in sequence.
