Weather and Going Out

01 Weather cards and basic meaning
What we will learn: We will learn how to identify common weather conditions and connect each condition to a natural going-out situation.
sunny

It's sunny.

Good weather for going out, meeting up, or getting some fresh air.

Use: Standard and very common in American and British English.

cloudy

It's cloudy.

The sky has many clouds. People can still go out, but the weather may change.

Use: Standard, clear, and very common for basic weather talk.

rainy

It's rainy.

There is rain. People often use umbrellas, stay in, or change outdoor plans.

Use: Correct and natural. For rain happening now, It's raining is more common.

pouring

It's pouring.

This means it is raining very hard. A game or outdoor plan may be called off.

hot / boiling

02 Weather questions and answers
What we will learn: We will learn the two most common questions for asking about weather and the sentence pattern used in answers.

Main questions

How's the weather?

What's the weather like?

Is it raining?

Main answer pattern

English commonly uses It is / It's for weather.

It's + weather adjective/expression.

It's sunny today.

It's raining right now.

It's freezing outside.

Topic cluster 2

Present Continuous

Present continuous explains actions that are happening now or around the present moment.

Open topics
03 Actions happening now
What we will learn: We will learn how to recognize present continuous sentences and understand why they are used for current activities.

Meaning

Use present continuous when an action is in progress now or around the present time.

Subject + am / is / are + verb-ing

Examples in context

I am checking the weather right now.

She is playing soccer.

They are hanging out at the park.

It is raining right now.

04 Negative form, questions, and short answers
What we will learn: We will learn how present continuous changes in negative sentences, questions, and short answers.

Negative

I am not watching the game right now.
She isn't playing today.
They aren't meeting up now.

Questions

Are you checking the weather?
What are you doing?
Is he warming up?

Short answers

Yes, I am.
No, I'm not.
Yes, she is.
No, they aren't.

Signal words

now, right now, today, at the moment, this week.

Topic cluster 3

Simple Present vs. Present Continuous

Students compare habits and current actions so they can understand the time meaning of each form.

Open topics
06 Habits compared with now
What we will learn: We will learn how to identify whether a sentence talks about a routine or an action happening now.

Simple Present

Use it for habits, routines, and general facts.

I play soccer every Saturday.She goes running on Mondays.

Present Continuous

Use it for actions in progress now or around now.

I am playing soccer now.She is running right now.

07 Sports and activity verb patterns
What we will learn: We will learn the verb pattern English speakers normally use with sports, exercise, and outdoor activities.

play + sport/game

I play soccer.
She plays basketball.
They play tennis.

go + -ing activity

We go running.
He goes swimming.
They go cycling.

do + activity

I do yoga.
She does exercise.
They do karate.

work out

This phrasal verb means to exercise or train.
She works out three times a week.

Phrasal Verbs and Idioms in Context

Open topics
08 Phrasal verbs for going out
What we will learn: We will learn the Spanish meaning, usage explanation, and context examples for the most useful phrasal verbs in this topic.
phrasal verb

go out

salir

Leave home for a social activity. We are going out tonight.

phrasal verb

hang out

pasar el rato

Spend informal time with friends. They are hanging out at the park.

phrasal verb

meet up

encontrarse / reunirse

Meet someone at an agreed place. We are meeting up after class.

phrasal verb

come over

venir a la casa de alguien

Go to someone's home or current place. Do you want to come over tonight?

phrasal verb

stay in

quedarse en casa

Not go out; remain at home. It's pouring, so we are staying in.

phrasal verb

call off

cancelar

Cancel a plan, event, game, or activity. They called off the game because of the rain.

09 Real weather and going-out idioms
What we will learn: We will learn real idioms connected to weather, plans, and going out. These expressions are not translated word by word.
idiom

It's raining cats and dogs.

está lloviendo a cántaros

Informal and playful. Use it for very heavy rain, not for formal weather reports. We can't play outside; it's raining cats and dogs.

idiom

take a rain check

dejarlo para otra ocasión

Use it to politely postpone a plan. It does not need to be raining. Can we take a rain check and meet tomorrow?

idiom

come rain or shine

pase lo que pase

Use it when a plan will happen no matter the situation. We are going to practice soccer, come rain or shine.

idiom

under the weather

sentirse mal / estar indispuesto

Use it when someone feels sick or low energy. I'm staying in because I feel under the weather.

idiom

every cloud has a silver lining

no hay mal que por bien no venga

Use it when something good can come from a bad situation. The game was canceled, but every cloud has a silver lining: we can watch a movie.

idiom

when it rains, it pours

las desgracias no vienen solas

Use it when several problems happen at the same time. First it started raining, then my phone died. When it rains, it pours.

Language Model

Conversation model for reading and analysis

A: What's the weather like?

B: It's sunny, but it's a little windy.

A: What are you doing now?

B: I'm warming up for a soccer game.

A: Are you going out after the game?

B: Yes. I'm meeting up with my friends. I think we are going to have a blast.
