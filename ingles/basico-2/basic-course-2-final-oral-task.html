Travel and Tourism

Two students. A trip to plan. A real conversation.

Work in pairs · At least 2 minutes for the whole pair

20% of your course grade · Each student receives an individual grade.

Choose your partnerLearn from the example

Your final oral task

A tourist wants to plan a trip. A travel assistant describes destinations and explains what people can and can’t do there. Your conversation can include any destination. At the end, the tourist chooses where to go or what to do and explains why. Save that final decision in your pair’s workspace.

Student A · Travel assistant

Introduce the destination. Describe what is there. Explain activities with You can… and You can’t…. Listen to your partner and give clear answers.

Student B · Tourist

Say what you like. Ask What can I do there? and Can I…? Ask for details or repetition. Finish by choosing an activity or place and explaining why.

Agree on your roles before the exam: one travel assistant and one tourist. Each student can prepare their own role. Both students speak and respond to each other. The conversation must last at least 2 minutes in total for the pair. You can use short notes and visual support.

Build your conversation in four steps

1 · Start

Greet each other and introduce the destination.

“Hello. I’d like to visit…”

2 · Describe

Talk about the place and possible activities.

“There is…” “You can visit…”

3 · Interact

Ask and answer questions. Follow your partner’s answer.

“Can I…?” “Yes, you can.”

4 · Decide

The tourist makes a choice and gives a reason.

“I want to… because…”

Use simple present and basic travel vocabulary. The picture helps you explain your ideas. Images and slides support your conversation; avoid slides full of sentences to read aloud.

Example · A trip to Cartagena

Listen to both voices. Notice how the tourist reacts and asks new questions. Then close the example and practise with your own ideas.

Pronunciation · Help your partner understand

Listen → repeat → say it to your partner. Practise at 0.75×, then at 1×. Clear speech matters more than copying an accent.

Can and can’t

can /kən/: often short and unstressed in “You can visit”.
can’t /kænt/: make the negative word clear. Your partner must hear the difference.

Stress the useful words: “You can VISIT the OLD TOWN.” “You CAN’T visit every place in one afternoon.”

Speak in small groups of words

“In Cartagena / you can visit the old town / and take photos.”

Keep final sounds clear in visit, beach, park and can’t. Pause between ideas. Ask “Can you repeat that, please?” when you need help.

Before the exam · Pronunciation checklist
  • I can say my destination clearly.
  • My partner can hear the difference between can and can’t.
  • I stress important words and pause between ideas.
  • I ask and answer without reading a full text.
  • I can ask for repetition and try again.
