01

Greetings, Polite Answers, and Farewells

Students learn the social frame of a first conversation: say hello, ask how the other person is, respond politely, and close the interaction.

Real context: first contact

Students use Unit 1 language when they arrive to class, meet a teacher, register their information, or introduce themselves to a new classmate. The language must be short, polite, and clear.

Greeting + name + polite answer + farewell

Formal and neutral greetings

Good morning.Use before noon.
Good afternoon.Use after noon.
Good evening.Use when you arrive at night.
Hello.Safe in almost every situation.

Informal and classroom language

Hi.Friendly and common.
How are you?Ask politely after greeting.
I am fine, thank you. And you?A complete beginner answer.
Goodbye. / Bye.Use when you leave.
Important: Good night is usually a farewell, not a greeting. When you arrive somewhere at night, say Good evening.

Formal / neutral

Good morning. / Hello. / Nice to meet you.

Use these with teachers, new classmates, and people you do not know well.

Informal

Hi. / Bye. / See you later.

Use these with friends, close classmates, and relaxed conversations.

02

The Verb To Be Inside Unit 1

The verb to be is not an isolated grammar page here. It is the engine students need to say who they are, where they are from, how old they are, and how they feel.

I am

Use I am to talk about yourself.

I am David. I am twenty years old. I am from Colombia.

He / She / It is

Use is for one person, one thing, or one place.

She is Ana. He is from Medellín. It is my email.

You / We / They are

Use are with you and plural subjects.

You are my classmate. We are students. They are in class.
FunctionStructureExampleSpanish idea
Name / identitySubject + be + nameMy name is Laura. / I am Laura.ser / llamarse
AgeSubject + be + ageI am twenty-one years old.tener años, but in English use be
OriginSubject + be + from + placeShe is from Envigado.ser de
FeelingSubject + be + adjectiveI am fine. He is happy.estar

Affirmative form

Use the subject first, then the correct form of be.

I am Ana. She is my teacher. They are classmates.

Contractions

Contractions sound natural in speaking and informal writing.

I am → I'm You are → You're He is / She is → He's / She's

Negative form

Use not after the verb be.

I am not from Bogotá. She is not my classmate. They are not in class.

Yes / No questions

Move am / is / are before the subject.

Are you a student?Yes, I am. / No, I am not.
Is she from Colombia?Yes, she is. / No, she is not.
Are they classmates?Yes, they are. / No, they are not.

Information questions

Use a question word first, then be, then the subject.

What is your name?My name is Laura.
Where are you from?I am from Colombia.
How old are you?I am twenty-one years old.
Common Mistake

In English we say I am 21 years old, not I have 21 years. Age uses the verb to be.

03

Personal Information

Students learn how to give basic information in complete, short sentences. This prepares them for registration forms, first-day conversations, and short oral introductions.

Real context: forms and introductions

Personal information is not only grammar. Students need it to complete forms, speak to a teacher, introduce themselves, and confirm contact information clearly.

My name is + age + city + phone number + email

Information to give

My name is Daniel.Name.
My last name is Rivera.Last name.
I am twenty-two years old.Age.
I am from Medellín.City or origin.
My phone number is 300 555 2841.Contact information.
My email is daniel.rivera@example.com.Email address.

Classroom model

Hello. My name is Daniel Rivera. I am twenty-two years old. I am from Medellín, but I live in Envigado. My phone number is 300 555 2841. My email is daniel.rivera@example.com. Nice to meet you.

NicknameapodoMy nickname is Ani.
Colombian context: many students have two first names and two last names. In English, first name usually means the given name, and last name usually means the family name used in the form.
04

Alphabet, Spelling, and Emails

Students need the alphabet immediately because personal information often includes names, last names, emails, usernames, and document numbers.

Real context: exact information

Spelling is necessary when the listener needs the exact letters of a name, last name, username, or email address. In Unit 1, spelling supports personal information.

How do you spell your last name? → R-I-V-E-R-A
NamesHow do you spell your name?

Use this when the listener needs exact letters.

A-L-E-X
Last namesCould you spell your last name?

This sounds polite in registration contexts.

R-I-V-E-R-A

05

Numbers for Personal Information

In Unit 1, numbers are primarily functional: age, phone numbers, document numbers, classroom quantity, and basic forms.

Cardinal numbers

Use cardinal numbers to count and give exact information.

I am twenty-one.Age.
My phone number is 300 555 2841.Contact.
There are ten students.Quantity.

Ordinal numbers preview

Ordinal numbers are not the main focus of Unit 1, but students see them in course language and dates.

Unit 1Say: unit one. Use a cardinal number.
the first classUse an ordinal number for order.
AgeI am twenty-one years old.

Use be, not have, for age.

Phonethree zero zero / five five five / two eight four one

For phone numbers, say digits clearly, often in groups.

IDMy ID number is 45 459 231.

Document numbers must be slow and exact.

Common number contrasts

thirteen / thirty13 vs 30
fourteen / forty14 vs 40
fifteen / fifty15 vs 50

Zero in contact information

For phone numbers, people often say zero. In casual speech, some speakers also say oh, but zero is clearer for beginners.

300 = three zero zero

Integrated Model Dialogue

This model shows the complete Unit 1 package in action: greeting, name, age, city, spelling, and polite closing.

First day registration

Sara: Nice to meet you, Alex. How do you spell your last name?

Alex: R-I-V-E-R-A.

Sara: Thank you. How old are you?

Alex: I am twenty-one years old.

Sara: Where are you from?

Alex: I am from Medellín, but I live in Envigado.

Sara: Great. Have a nice class.

Alex: Thank you. Have a great day.
