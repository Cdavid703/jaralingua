01

People and Identity

Start with the most basic information: who the person is, where the person is from, and what the person does.

Real context: identify one person

In this unit, students describe famous people, classmates, suspects, and family members. The first step is simple identity language.

She is a singer. He is my classmate. They are from Colombia.

He

Use he for one male person.

He is my brother.He is an actor.

She

Use she for one female person.

She is my aunt.She is a singer.

They

Use they for two or more people.

They are my parents.They are soccer players.

02

Describing People

Students need description language for Guess Who, family photos, favorite people, and eyewitness-style speaking tasks.

Be + adjective

Use is/are before adjectives.

She is friendly.He is tall.They are young.

Have/has + feature

Use has for he/she and have for they.

She has long hair.He has glasses.They have brown eyes.

Adjective + noun

In English, the adjective usually goes before the noun.

03

Verb To Be Review for Descriptions

Unit 3 still depends on the verb to be. Students use it to identify people and describe them.

SubjectAffirmativeNegativeQuestionShort answer
II am a student.I am not famous.Am I late?Yes, I am. / No, I am not.
He / SheShe is my sister.He is not old.Is she your cousin?Yes, she is. / No, she is not.
TheyThey are my friends.They are not actors.Are they your parents?Yes, they are. / No, they are not.
Core idea: use be for identity and adjectives. Use has/have for physical features like hair, glasses, or clothes.
04

Yes/No Questions About People

Yes/no questions help students play Guess Who, check family relationships, and confirm descriptions.

With be

Is she your sister?Are they your classmates?

Move is/are before the subject.

With adjectives

Is he tall?Are they friendly?

Use this for physical or personality descriptions.

Short answers

Yes, she is.No, they are not.

Do not repeat the full sentence every time.

Guess Who pattern

Ask about one person and eliminate options.

Is he wearing glasses?Is she friendly?Is he your favorite actor?

Family photo pattern

Ask about people in a photo.

Is she your mother?Are they your grandparents?Is he your cousin?

07

Talking About a Family Photo

Family-photo language connects vocabulary, pronouns, possessives, and yes/no questions.

Photo description model

Students should describe people from left to right or from the main person outward. This creates order and avoids random sentences.

This is my mother. Her name is Ana. She is friendly. These are my grandparents. Their names are Rosa and Luis.

One person near me

This is my sister.She is twenty.

One person far from me

That is my uncle.He is funny.

Two or more people

These are my parents.They are kind.

Useful questions

Who is the person in the photo?Is she your mother?Are they your grandparents?

Useful answers

Yes, she is. She is my mother.No, he is not. He is my uncle.Yes, they are. They are my parents.
08

Likes and Favorite People

Students can add one or two simple likes to make a favorite-person description more personal.

I like

Use base verb after I like.

I like music.I like my sister.

He likes / She likes

Add -s with he and she.

He likes soccer.She likes reading.

Favorite person

Explain who the person is and why the person is important.

My favorite person is my mother because she is kind.
Keep it basic: Unit 3 does not need long present-simple grammar yet. Use likes only to enrich short descriptions.
09

Main Model and Final Product

The final model integrates identity, family vocabulary, possessives, adjectives, and one simple reason.

Final speaking model: My favorite person

My favorite person is my sister. Her name is Sofia. She is twenty years old. She is friendly, smart, and funny. She has long hair and brown eyes. She likes music and movies. She is important to me because she helps me and listens to me.
